## Method and status key

This ledger records a fresh, chronological, result-by-result audit against
`AUTHORING.md`, the earlier books, and the local Mathlib checkout. A book is
marked **PASS** only after every line from the title through EOF has been read
without tool-output elision and every substantive result has an available
proof or prerequisite. **FAIL** means that the full read is complete but at
least one operative gap remains. **PENDING** means that no full-read
attestation is made yet. Repairs and dependency defects are recorded with
locations and evidence below.

## Confirmed cross-book findings

### 1. Cotangent-amplitude converse is not established

- **Source / consumer:** Book 065, Section 7.2, especially lines 866--920; consumed immediately by the lci converse at lines 928--942 and by any later argument that infers a regular sequence from cotangent amplitude.
- **Needed result:** for a quotient $B=P/I$ of a noetherian local ring, perfection of $L_{B/P}$ with amplitude $[-1,-1]$ implies that $I$ is generated by a regular sequence (the deep complete-intersection/cotangent criterion).
- **Gap:** lines 896--903 introduce a filtration spectral sequence and then assert that its derived-symmetric-power layers have “no further differential or extension.” Freeness of $I/I^2$ and the identification $L_{B/P}\simeq(I/I^2)[1]$ do not by themselves prove spectral-sequence degeneration or identify the entire Tor algebra. Lines 913--920 therefore cannot yet compare the Koszul and minimal resolutions as claimed.
- **Availability check:** searches in the local checkout find only the naive presentation cotangent complex and standard-smooth results (`RingTheory/Extension/Cotangent/*` and `RingTheory/Smooth/StandardSmoothCotangent.lean`), not this full cotangent-complex complete-intersection converse. No earlier manuscript result has been identified that proves the missing degeneration/theorem.
- **Chronological repair:** insert a rigorous proof of the complete-intersection cotangent criterion before Book 065's lci converse (or make it a fully proved earlier prerequisite), then cite that theorem at lines 896--920. Merely naming the Quillen criterion would violate the project rule.

### 2. Quaternionic strong approximation proof uses a non-global direction

- **Source / consumers:** Book 118, Section 6.2 (strong approximation for the quaternionic norm-one group); its component calculation is consumed in Book 120 and downstream Shimura-curve component/Hecke arguments.
- **Needed result:** density/strong approximation for the simply connected norm-one group of a quaternion algebra away from a split place, with the exact finite-adelic formulation used in the component quotient.
- **Gap:** the displayed proof chooses an isotropic pair over the local completion and then varies a purportedly global scalar in that local isotropic direction, although that direction need not be defined over the global field. Its denominator-clearing step also asks CRT to avoid every “new prime divisor” of a quadratic denominator; those primes depend on the element being chosen, so the finite CRT argument is circular and does not prove the denominator is an $S$-unit.
- **Availability check:** the local checkout has no theorem matching quaternionic/algebraic-group strong approximation (a repository search for `strong approximation` supplies no such result), and no earlier assigned manuscript has yet been found to prove this theorem.
- **Chronological repair:** establish the required simply connected strong-approximation theorem, with a valid global-to-local proof, in an earlier book before 118; then replace Section 6.2's argument by a citation and carry its exact hypotheses into Books 120 and later consumers.

### 3. Lenstra's equality-implies-complete-intersection step is only sketched

- **Source / consumers:** Book 071, Section 7.3, especially lines 1157--1196 (the asserted filtered determinant implication (7.3.1)); consumed by the relation--determinant lemma at lines 1128--1210, Lenstra's complete-intersection characterization at lines 1212--1235, the source-unrestricted Wiles--Lenstra theorem in Section 8, Book 071's later reusable/deformation--Hecke criteria, and the abstract $R=T$ argument in Book 146, especially Sections 9.6 and 16.2.
- **Needed result:** if $B=S/(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$ is the complete-intersection parameter reduction constructed there and $A=B/L$ remains finite flat, then a nonzero extra kernel $L$ forces a strict enlargement $\eta_B\subsetneq\eta_A$; equivalently, equality of the cotangent Fitting ideal and branch congruence ideal forces the original presentation ideal to have exactly height-many generators.
- **Gap:** the text invokes an unspecified “comparison” of the Koszul complexes, says that a first associated-graded class of $L$ has a nonzero Koszul boundary, and asserts that exterior multiplication carries it to a nonzero class in $\operatorname{Ann}_A(I_A)/\operatorname{Ann}_B(I_B)$. No chain maps, filtered complexes, associated-graded differential, or map from that boundary to the annihilator quotient are defined, and the claim that a hypothetical later boundary would yield $\varpi^c b\in L$ with $b\notin L$ is asserted without derivation. Artin--Rees and $\varpi$-saturation alone do not supply these missing identifications. Thus (7.3.1), the only step excluding higher-order extra relations, has not been proved.
- **Availability check:** the local Mathlib checkout contains no Wiles--Lenstra numerical criterion, congruence-ideal relation--determinant theorem, or equivalent complete-intersection converse (searches for `Lenstra`, `Wiles.*criterion`, `congruence ideal`, and related complete-intersection/cotangent phrases find only unrelated Galois-category references). Books 064--070 provide presentations, depth, regular sequences, Fitting ideals, and the forward complete-intersection calculation, but not this determinant-defect theorem.
- **Chronological repair:** replace lines 1157--1196 with a complete proof of the determinant-defect theorem, defining the comparison complexes and every induced map and proving strictness, before stating the Section 7.3 characterization. Only then may Section 8 and Book 146 invoke the source-unrestricted Wiles--Lenstra criterion.

### 4. Principal-series reducibility is inferred from an unproved boundary-submodule lemma

The shell ratios $qz$ and $q^{-1}z$ locate plausible exceptional exponents but do not establish the boundary-submodule lemma.
- **Availability check:** the local Mathlib checkout contains no principal-series, Jacquet-module, or $p$-adic $\operatorname{GL}_2$ reducibility theorem, and Books 001--072 do not prove this group-specific classification.
- **Chronological repair:** insert a complete rank-one reducibility proof in Book 073 before Theorem 7.1, for example by explicitly classifying invariant subspaces in a valid model or by proving the standard intertwiner kernel/image theorem at every specialization. Then rederive the two nonsplit exceptional sequences and all special-representation consumers.

### 5. Whittaker uniqueness and Kirillov cyclicity use an unproved distribution criterion

- **Source / consumers:** Book 073, Lemma 8.1 and Theorem 8.2, especially lines 1214--1301, with a further unsupported “mirabolic cyclicity calculation” in Section 9.1; consumed by the claimed canonical Whittaker model, Kirillov injectivity/core/asymptotic quotient, spherical Whittaker formula, local zeta ideals and functional equations in the rest of Book 073, and later character/transfer arguments in Books 074--076 and beyond.
- **Needed result:** multiplicity one $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_N(\pi,\psi)\le1$ for irreducible admissible $\operatorname{GL}_2(F)$ representations, together with the mirabolic cyclicity statement needed for restriction to the diagonal to be injective.
- **Gap:** the proof treats a distribution on the open Bruhat cell as though finite Fourier projection automatically decomposed it as a tensor product, and passes from open- and closed-cell restrictions to the whole distribution without handling distributions supported on the boundary or extension terms. The stated Gelfand--Kazhdan criterion is then “proved” by unspecified regularizations and operators: no kernel distribution is defined from two functionals, no compatibility across levels is proved, and no reason is given that the resulting operators commute with the full integrated $G$-action. Section 9.1 similarly invokes finite Fourier inversion to express $w\lambda$ by mirabolic translates without exhibiting the identity. These are the substantive multiplicity-one and cyclicity steps, not formal consequences of admissibility.
- **Availability check:** searches of the local Mathlib checkout find no Whittaker, Gelfand--Kazhdan, mirabolic cyclicity, or $p$-adic $\operatorname{GL}_2$ multiplicity-one theorem; earlier books supply only the abstract smooth-representation framework.
- **Chronological repair:** prove a precise distributional Gelfand--Kazhdan theorem with the required topology/support filtration and its multiplicity-one deduction, then prove mirabolic cyclicity, before defining the Kirillov model as an injective model or using uniqueness in local-factor and transfer calculations.

### 6. The tame dihedral type, conductor, and local-constant package is not proved

- **Needed result:** a complete construction of tame positive-depth $\operatorname{GL}_2(F)$ types from admissible quadratic pairs, exact intertwining/support-reduction and fixed-level calculations, and the conductor/newvector and epsilon-induction formulas with the stated rectifier.
- **Gap:** the “successive approximation” in Theorem 7.1 never defines the graded lattices and intersection domains needed to deduce that an arbitrary intertwiner lies in $E^\times J^1$, while its support-reduction and fixed-level parts merely repeat the expected minimal-distance argument without constructing the projectors or proving a value survives. Lemma 10.1 similarly asserts a complete Mackey double-coset classification and one-dimensional Hom calculation from two informal root bounds; it does not calculate the intersections or the Heisenberg invariant spaces. The rectifier lemma postulates a finite Gauss-ratio identity and says “complete the square” without evaluating the sums or proving the three functorial identities, and Theorem 12.1 only says that unfolding turns the Weyl transform into Fourier transform on $E$, without displaying the unfolding or measure comparison. These are precisely the deep type/conductor/local-constant theorems being claimed.
- **Availability check:** the local Mathlib checkout has no Bushnell--Kutzko simple-character/type theory, tame $\operatorname{GL}_2$ supercuspidal classification, newvector theorem, rectifier, or automorphic-induction epsilon formula; earlier manuscripts supply only abstract compact induction and the already-gapped Book 073 Whittaker framework.
- **Chronological repair:** replace these sketches with a complete tame type theory in Book 074, including explicit lattice filtrations, intertwining domains, Mackey representatives and Hom spaces, finite Gauss sums, and measure-normalized unfolding. Only after those proofs should Books 076 and 080 use the dihedral classification, conductor, newvector, character, or epsilon packages.

### 7. General Weil local constants are not constructed from the asserted Brauer relations

- **Source / consumers:** Book 075, Theorem 10.1 and its proof strategy, especially lines 1191--1267; consumed by the Weil--Deligne epsilon and gamma factors in Sections 10.3--10.5, every duality/twist/induction formula in Section 11, the functional-equation package in Section 12, and the local-correspondence comparisons in Books 076 and 080.
- **Needed result:** existence and uniqueness of epsilon factors for every finite-inertia Weil representation, including a proof that the product of rank-one local constants attached to an integral degree-zero monomial-induction expression is independent of the chosen expression and satisfies dimension-zero induction.
- **Gap:** Book 003, Proposition 11.1B proves integral monomial induction for finite-group characters, but Book 075 requires both an augmentation-ideal refinement and the compatibility of rank-one Fourier constants with every relation among those induced characters. Lines 1227--1235 merely say to apply an unnamed “elementary-subgroup character criterion” or repeat a denominator-removal proof; they do not derive the degree-zero expression (10.3b) from Book 003. More decisively, lines 1250--1256 posit two unspecified stable fractional ideals and claim that Fourier-transform determinants on isotypic components are the primitive Gauss scalars and multiply to one for every virtual relation. The finite function spaces, determinant lines, component identifications, exact sequences, and conductor-normalized comparison are not defined or proved. - **Availability check:** the local Mathlib checkout has no theorem constructing Weil epsilon factors or proving compatibility of local constants with Brauer induction. Earlier Book 003 supplies integral monomial induction and conductor induction, while Books 073--074 contain only rank-one or special/dihedral calculations (and themselves have the gaps recorded above); none proves the required relation-independence theorem.
- **Chronological repair:** prove the degree-zero Brauer induction statement and Deligne's relation-independence theorem in full before definition (10.3c), with explicit finite Fourier spaces, determinant comparisons, trace/self-dual-measure normalizations, and induction compatibility. Then derive Theorem 10.1 and only afterward use general Weil--Deligne local constants in Books 076 and 080.
