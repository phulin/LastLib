Brauer induction solves a descent problem by allowing subtraction. The preceding book produced,
for every coefficient embedding, a signed expression

$$
\mathcal R_\tau=[R_\tau^+]-[R_\tau^-]
$$

whose virtual rank is two, whose determinant line is cyclotomic, and whose good Frobenius series
is a quadratic polynomial independent of the coefficient embedding. None of those assertions
says that the negative constituents of $R_\tau^-$ occur in $R_\tau^+$. Effectivity is exactly
the missing assertion.

The argument needed downstream is coefficientwise at the selected place $\nu\mid3$ from Book
183, Theorem 11.1. The same character-norm calculation has an optional all-coefficient form;
at coefficient characteristic two that stronger form uses the explicit top-packet
irreducibility hypothesis $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ stated in Section 1.1. The central idea is not to inspect one
Frobenius polynomial at a time. We
instead measure the signed character globally by its character norm. Mackey theory and
Frobenius reciprocity turn that norm into a finite Gram-matrix calculation among the induced
summands. Book 183 identifies the two restrictions on every elementary intersection field with
one selected cuspidal packet, so every entry is a self-twist calculation. The possible
intertwining is therefore a finite-order self-twist question. Its answer is algebraic and is
unchanged by a coefficient embedding once the relevant packet realization is known to be
absolutely irreducible. At the distinguished embedding the virtual class is the given
absolutely irreducible representation, so its norm is one. Book 183 supplies the needed
irreducibility unconditionally for the selected embedding inducing $\nu\mid3$. Its optional
uniform packet certificate supplies it at every coefficient embedding away from two, and the
coefficient-two extension additionally uses $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ or one of the exact finite gates
stated below.

An integral generalized character of norm one has one irreducible coefficient equal to $1$ or
$-1$ and all others zero. Its virtual dimension is two, so the sign must be positive and the
irreducible constituent must have dimension two. This short final step is why all the earlier
bookkeeping matters: integrality, not merely rationality, turns a numerical norm into
positivity.

The resulting characteristic-zero effectivity produces an actual continuous irreducible
semisimple representation and promotes the virtual determinant and local records to records of
that representation. It does not canonically subtract lattices, choose coefficient-field
models simultaneously at every place, or construct the base-field compatible system. Those
coherent global tasks belong to Book 185.

There are three coefficient scopes. The main theorem is coefficientwise at the selected
$\nu\mid3$. Under Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate, the same argument applies at
every coefficient embedding away from two. An all-coefficient statement additionally retains
the coefficient-two gate. The fixed-three Fermat route uses only the first scope, so it is
conditional on the controlled top datum and its stated upstream hypotheses, but not on either
the uniform certificate or $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$. Nothing in that route can be used backward to
produce the optional family or its coefficient-two member.

Possible sources for $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ belong to the optional uniform theory. They are outside
the selected-three theorem and unnecessary for the fixed-three contradiction.

**Interface received.** Book 183, Theorem 11.1, Proposition 6.2, and equations
(6.4e)--(6.4h) export the
exact comparison required here. For every pair of elementary subgroups $H,J$, every
$x\in\Gamma$, and

$$
D=H\cap xJx^{-1},\qquad L=M^D=F_H\,x(F_J),
$$

the two packet realizations restrict semisimply to the one realization $r_{D,\tau}$ over
$G_L$, for every coefficient embedding $\tau$. This remains true when either extension from an
elementary fixed field to $L$ is non-Galois. Book 183 also exports the two restricted Brauer
characters $\alpha$, $\beta$, their correctly oriented ratio
$\delta=\beta\alpha^{-1}$, and the resulting Hom-ready identity. The comparison is
characteristic zero at every embedding. No packet lattice, integral overlap map, or coherent
choice of overlap intertwiners is part of the export or needed below.

## 1. The effectivity problem

### 1.1 The package received from Book 183

We begin by naming exactly what is available. Fix the controlled totally real Galois extension

$$
M/\mathbf Q,\qquad \Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbf Q),
$$

and the absolutely irreducible distinguished representation

$$
\rho:G_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(E),
\qquad \det\rho=\chi_\ell.
\tag{1.1}
$$

Their arithmetic provenance is fixed upstream: Book 178, Theorem 12.2 supplies the restricted
signed-special finite fibre and retained support, while Book 182, Proposition 7.1 and Theorem
9.1 supply the selected-three packets and their signed-special realizations. This book uses
that selected output through Book 183, Theorem 11.1.

Here $E/\mathbf Q_\ell$ is finite, $\ell\geq7$, and no lattice is part of the input. The
restriction $\rho|_{G_M}$ is absolutely irreducible; consequently every
$\rho|_{G_{F_H}}$ is absolutely irreducible as well. The primes $2$ and $\ell$ split
completely in $M$.

At $2$, the chosen representation has Book 183's special, or SP, record. In arithmetic-Frobenius
normalization its Frobenius-semisimple Weil--Deligne pair is

$$
\mathscr D_2=(r_2,N_2),
\qquad
N_2^2=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}N_2=1.
\tag{1.1a}
$$

Thus $N_2\ne0$ and

$$
\mathscr L_2=\ker N_2=\operatorname{im}N_2
\tag{1.1b}
$$

is a line. Finite Weil inertia is trivial, and

$$
r_2(F_2)N_2r_2(F_2)^{-1}=2N_2.
\tag{1.1c}
$$

For a fixed sign $\varepsilon_2\in\{1,-1\}$, arithmetic Frobenius acts on the quotient
by $\varepsilon_2$ and on $\mathscr L_2$ by $2\varepsilon_2$. In particular the SP label
contains the nonzero monodromy, its invariant line, and its sign; conductor one will be
recovered from this full pair rather than assumed as a substitute for it.

For every elementary subgroup $H\leq\Gamma$, put

$$
F_H=M^H.
$$

Book 183, using the selected packets exported by Book 182, Theorem 9.1, supplies a selected
parallel-weight-two cuspidal packet $\pi_H$ over $F_H$. The packets
are conjugation compatible, their base changes to $M$ are the one selected packet $\pi_M$, and
at the distinguished coefficient embedding their Galois realizations satisfy

$$
r_{H,\tau_0}\simeq\rho|_{G_{F_H}}.
\tag{1.2}
$$

The selection includes determinant, finite-order twists, coefficient embeddings, good-prime
polynomials, and the algebraic SP local pair. It includes no packet lattice. Book 183's
elementary-intersection theorem adds the exact overlap statement needed by the norm argument.
If $D\leq H$ are elementary, then
at every coefficient embedding $\tau$,

$$
\left(r_{H,\tau}|_{G_{F_D}}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq r_{D,\tau}.
\tag{1.3}
$$

More generally, for elementary $H,J\leq\Gamma$ and $x\in\Gamma$, put

$$
D=H\cap xJx^{-1},\qquad L=M^D=F_H\,x(F_J).
$$

Applying (1.3) to $D\leq H$ and to $D\leq xJx^{-1}$, then using conjugation compatibility,
gives

$$
\left(r_{H,\tau}|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}
\simeq r_{D,\tau}
\simeq
\left({}^x r_{J,\tau}|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}.
\tag{1.3a}
$$

No normality of $D$ in $H$ or $xJx^{-1}$ is required. Book 183 proves (1.3) by comparing the
correct residue-degree transforms of the two algebraic Hecke polynomials at the distinguished
embedding, using injectivity to obtain equality in the common number field, and applying its
Chebotarev--Brauer--Nesbitt comparison to the two already existing representations over $F_D$.
Thus (1.3)--(1.3a) are imported theorems, not assumptions and not applications of an
automorphic base-change theorem along the possibly non-Galois intersection extension.

For Brauer characters $\theta_H,\theta_J$, Book 183 further retains

$$
\alpha=\theta_H|_D,\qquad
\beta=({}^x\theta_J)|_D,\qquad
\delta=\beta\alpha^{-1}.
\tag{1.3b}
$$

The two twisted restrictions are respectively $r_{D,\tau}\otimes\alpha_\tau$ and
$r_{D,\tau}\otimes\beta_\tau$. After tensoring both Hom arguments by $\alpha_\tau^{-1}$,
their Hom space is the self-twist space from $r_{D,\tau}$ to
$r_{D,\tau}\otimes\delta_\tau$. This direction fixes the ratio as
$\beta\alpha^{-1}$, not its inverse. The roots of unity in these three characters lie in the
common coefficient field. The rank-two comparisons in (1.3)--(1.3a) remain
characteristic-zero comparisons at every $\tau$ and produce no integral intertwiner or
coherent system of intertwiners.

For the selected embedding inducing $\nu\mid3$, Book 183's full SP pair proves the absolute
irreducibility needed below. The same assertion at other coefficient places away from two
requires Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate. An all-place extension additionally
needs one named coefficient-two gate. Write $q(\tau)$ for the residue characteristic of the
nonarchimedean target of $\tau$. For that optional extension, assume explicitly that

$$
\boxed{
r_{1,\tau}:G_M\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(\Omega)
\text{ is absolutely irreducible whenever }q(\tau)=2.}
\tag{$\mathrm{AI}_2$}
$$

Here $r_{1,\tau}$ is the realization of the top packet $\pi_M$. This is a rational
characteristic-zero hypothesis, not a residual or lattice assertion. It is a standing
hypothesis below, but it is automatically supplied when the controlled datum uses
$(\mathrm{HT}_1)$: Book 183, Proposition 6.1 applies Book 168, Theorem 7.6 to the selected
cuspidal packet $\pi_M$ at every coefficient place. If the controlled datum instead uses only
direct prime-to-two local preservation or ambient semisimplicity, $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ remains
separate. Proposition 5.1
explains why this single top-field hypothesis suffices for every elementary intersection
packet.

For one fixed Brauer datum and one fixed coefficient-two embedding $\tau$, the logically exact
numerical gate for absolutely irreducible effectivity is

$$
\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\|^2=n^TG_\tau n=1
\qquad(q(\tau)=2).
\tag{$\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B)$}
$$

Indeed, the integral multiplicity and virtual-dimension argument of Chapter 7 shows that this
condition is equivalent to the desired positive absolutely irreducible rank-two class. For
relation independence the corresponding exact numerical gate is norm zero for the difference
of the two presentations. The dependence of $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ on the fixed
embedding $\tau$ is suppressed in the notation. The existing entrywise Gram proof uses the
following more structured, still finite replacement for $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$. For every triple
$(i,j,x)$ occurring in its Mackey calculation, with $D=D_{ij}(x)$, $L=M^D$, and
$\delta=\delta_{ij,x}$ as in (3.15)--(3.16), require

$$
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{G_L}\left(r_{D,\tau},r_{D,\tau}\otimes\delta_\tau\right)=

\begin{cases}
1,&\pi_D\otimes(\psi_\delta\circ\det)\simeq\pi_D,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\qquad(q(\tau)=2).
\tag{$\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B)$}
$$

For relation independence the same condition is required for the finite union of triples from
the two relations. Its dependence on $\tau$ is likewise suppressed. This finite Hom-dimension
condition is exactly what gives the existing Mackey Gram matrix proof coefficient-independent
entries; individual entry invariance is not logically necessary if the signed quadratic value
can be proved directly. Hypothesis $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ is the clean uniform sufficient hypothesis:
Proposition 5.1 gives absolute irreducibility of every $r_{D,\tau}$, and Proposition 5.2 then
gives $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ by Schur's lemma and strong multiplicity one. No converse is
claimed.

In particular, $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ is not an operation which completes an away-from-two family.
The elementary packet member $r_{1,\tau}$ at $q(\tau)=2$ already exists as a continuous
semisimple characteristic-zero representation by the packet construction of Books 127 and
168. What is missing is a proof of its absolute irreducibility, or directly of
$(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ for the present Gram proof, or at minimum of
$(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$. Common Frobenius polynomials in completions away from two supply
neither a scalar map to a two-adic completion nor continuity, positivity, or a
two-dimensional Cayley--Hamilton representation there. They recognize two representations
after both exist; they do not manufacture the effective base-field constituent needed here.

Choose an integral Brauer datum

$$
\mathfrak B=\{(H_i,\theta_i,n_i)\}_{i=1}^t,
\qquad
1_\Gamma=\sum_{i=1}^t n_i\operatorname{Ind}_{H_i}^{\Gamma}\theta_i,
\tag{1.4}
$$

where the $H_i$ are elementary, the $\theta_i$ are finite-order linear characters, and
$n_i\in\mathbf Z$. Put $F_i=F_{H_i}$. Use the common Galois number field $K$ of Book 183,
enlarged once for all to contain the Hecke eigenvalues, local-type values, and character values
for every elementary packet in the controlled finite family. In particular it contains the data
for all elementary intersections

$$
H_i\cap xH_jx^{-1}
\tag{1.5}
$$

that occur below. A relation-specific proof would need only this finite intersection list; the
once-for-all field makes comparisons between two Brauer data harmless. Enlarging it changes no
representation after faithful scalar extension.

For an embedding $\tau:K\hookrightarrow\Omega$ into an algebraically closed characteristic-zero
nonarchimedean field, define

$$
A_{i,\tau}=
\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{F_i}}^{G_{\mathbf Q}}
\bigl(r_{H_i,\tau}\otimes\theta_{i,\tau}\bigr)
\tag{1.6}
$$

and

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=\sum_i n_i[A_{i,\tau}].
\tag{1.7}
$$

All representations entering (1.6) are understood semisimply. Book 183 proves

$$
\dim\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=2,
\qquad
\det\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=\chi_\tau,
\tag{1.8}
$$

and, at the distinguished embedding $\tau_0$ extending $E\hookrightarrow\Omega$,

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau_0}=[\rho\otimes_E\Omega].
\tag{1.9}
$$

There is one finite set $S_0$ such that for $p\notin S_0$ and $p$ different from the residue
characteristic of $\tau$, the virtual Frobenius series is

$$
\det(1-\operatorname{Frob}_pT\mid\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau})
=1-\tau(a_p)T+pT^2
\tag{1.10}
$$

for one $a_p\in K$. Arithmetic Frobenius is used, so
$\chi_\tau(\operatorname{Frob}_p)=p$.

### 1.2 What has not yet been proved

Write $n_i=n_i^+-n_i^-$ with $n_i^\pm\geq0$ and set

$$
R_\tau^+=\bigoplus_i A_{i,\tau}^{\oplus n_i^+},
\qquad
R_\tau^-=\bigoplus_i A_{i,\tau}^{\oplus n_i^-}.
\tag{1.11}
$$

Then $\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=[R_\tau^+]-[R_\tau^-]$. To prove effectivity is to prove
that, after decomposing these semisimple representations into irreducibles, every constituent
of $R_\tau^-$ occurs in $R_\tau^+$ with at least the same multiplicity. Equivalently, there
must be an actual semisimple $V_\tau$ such that

$$
R_\tau^+\simeq V_\tau\oplus R_\tau^-.
\tag{1.12}
$$

The isomorphism in (1.12), if it exists, is not expected to be canonical.

Neither (1.8) nor (1.10) proves (1.12). Virtual dimension records one integer. The determinant
line records one character. Formula (1.10) records elementwise cancellations on a dense family
of conjugacy classes. Effectivity asks for simultaneous inequalities among every irreducible
multiplicity. The appropriate invariant is the norm of the entire generalized character.

### 1.3 The main theorem

**Theorem 1.1 (effectivity of the signed Brauer array).** Fix an integral Brauer datum
$\mathfrak B$.

- If $\tau$ induces the selected place $\nu\mid3$, the virtual class
  $\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ is represented by a continuous absolutely irreducible
  two-dimensional semisimple representation.
- Under Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate, the same conclusion holds for every
  $\tau$ with $q(\tau)\ne2$.
- If $q(\tau)=2$, the same absolutely irreducible effectivity conclusion holds if and only if
  $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ holds at $\tau$. The finite condition
  $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ implies this norm condition. The uniform hypothesis
  $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ implies $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ for every $\mathfrak B$ and every
  coefficient embedding of residue characteristic two.

Whenever the conclusion holds, write its unique isomorphism class as

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}:G_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(\Omega).
\tag{1.13}
$$

Whenever one of these hypotheses applies, it satisfies

$$
\det r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=\chi_\tau,
\qquad
\det(1-r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}(\operatorname{Frob}_p)T)
=1-\tau(a_p)T+pT^2
\tag{1.14}
$$

at every $p\notin S_0$ distinct from the residue characteristic of $\tau$; at such a prime
it is unramified. At $\tau_0$ it is $\rho$ after scalar extension. Its actual restriction at
every place satisfies the direct-sum Mackey cancellation of Section 9.2; away from the
coefficient residue characteristic, its Weil--Deligne class is the effective member of Book
183's local record. In particular, at $2$ and away from coefficient characteristic two it has
nonzero rank-one monodromy, the invariant line and sign in (1.1b)--(1.1c), and Artin conductor
exponent one. The representation is independent, after scalar extension, of the
positive-minus-negative presentation. It is independent, after scalar extension, of the
integral Brauer datum at the selected place and, under the optional uniform packet certificate,
throughout the away-from-two range; the all-coefficient version also uses
$(\mathrm{AI}_2)$. More generally, at one coefficient-two embedding, two Brauer data
which each satisfy their norm-one condition give isomorphic effective representations by their
common dense Frobenius polynomials and Brauer--Nesbitt.

The theorem asserts no preferred stable lattice for a general $\tau$. It also does not yet
assemble the representations (1.13), their coefficient fields, and their place labels into one
base-field compatible system. That assembly is the task of Book 185.

Thus $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ is not logically necessary for one fixed relation: the exact gate there
is norm one. It is the clean coefficient-two condition used for the optional all-coefficient
theorem. Without the optional uniform packet certificate, the unconditional conclusion remains
the selected member at $\nu\mid3$. With that certificate, the theorem holds away from two; a
coefficient-two conclusion is made only when $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$, or a stated
sufficient condition for it, has actually been supplied.

### 1.4 Proof architecture

The proof has five steps.

1. Define an integral inner product on virtual semisimple representations and observe that norm
   one forces exactly one coefficient $\pm1$.
2. Use Frobenius reciprocity and Mackey decomposition to express
   $\langle A_{i,\tau},A_{j,\tau}\rangle$ as a sum over double cosets.
3. Insert Book 183's overlap identities (1.3)--(1.3b), separately for every double coset, to
   turn every summand into the self-twist space of one cuspidal rank-two realization over its
   elementary intersection field.
4. Prove the self-twist formula at the selected $\nu\mid3$ and, under the optional uniform
   packet certificate, at every coefficient embedding away from two. At residue characteristic
   two, the identical conclusion follows from
   $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$, while $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ bypasses entrywise Gram
   invariance and states the needed quadratic value directly.
5. At $\tau_0$ the class is the absolutely irreducible $\rho$, so the common norm is one. Rank
   two fixes the sign. For a coefficient-two embedding under the minimal gate, norm one is
   already the hypothesis and the same integral sign argument applies.

Clifford theory explains structurally why this calculation is possible: every induced summand
lies in the same block above the top-field representation. It also identifies the only possible
ambiguity as a finite quotient self-twist, precisely the ambiguity measured in Step 3.

## 2. Generalized characters and their norm

Book 180, Section 7.1 is the general source for the semisimple Grothendieck group, its Hom
pairing, and the norm-one sign lemma. We keep the scalar-extension formula and the formulas used
by the SP application so that the later positivity check remains explicit.

### 2.1 The semisimple Grothendieck group

Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field,

$$
V\simeq\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha^{\oplus m_\alpha}, \tag{2.1}
$$

and every virtual class has a unique integral expansion

$$
X=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha[S_\alpha]. \tag{2.2}
$$

Its virtual dimension and generalized trace are

$$
\dim X=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\dim S_\alpha, \tag{2.3}
$$

$$
\chi_X(g)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\operatorname{tr}S_\alpha(g). \tag{2.4}
$$

### 2.2 The Hom inner product

Book 180 defines

$$
\langle V,W\rangle_G=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(V,W), \tag{2.5}
$$

with Schur orthogonality

$$
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(S_\alpha,S_\beta)=\delta_{\alpha\beta}. \tag{2.6}
$$

Thus

$$
\left\langle\sum c_\alpha[S_\alpha],
\sum d_\alpha[S_\alpha]\right\rangle=\sum c_\alpha d_\alpha, \tag{2.7}
$$

and

$$
\|X\|^2=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^2. \tag{2.8}
$$

For a finite group this equals the usual character average

$$
\langle\chi,\psi\rangle_G=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)\psi(g^{-1}). \tag{2.9}
$$

For the profinite Galois groups below, (2.5), not a Haar average, is the definition.

### 2.3 Norm one and the sign lemma

The Book 180 norm-one lemma says

$$
\|X\|^2=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X=[S]\text{ or }-[S] \tag{2.11}
$$

for one irreducible $S$. If $\dim X=d>0$, the sign is positive and $\dim S=d$. The integrality
of Book 183's Brauer relation is essential: the assertion is false for arbitrary rational
coefficient vectors.

### 2.4 Scalar extension

For a splitting extension $L'/L$,

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_G(V,W)\otimes_LL'
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_G(V\otimes_LL',W\otimes_LL'). \tag{2.12}
$$

Indeed, the image of the group algebra in
$\operatorname{End}_L(V\oplus W)$ is finite dimensional. Choose finitely many group elements
whose images span it. Equivariance is then the finite system of linear equations

$$
TV(g)-W(g)T=0, \tag{2.13}
$$

for those chosen elements, so flat scalar extension preserves the common kernel and hence the
Hom dimension. This justifies the single finite Galois coefficient ledger used in the Gram
calculation.

## 3. Frobenius reciprocity and the Mackey Gram matrix

### 3.1 Why the norm must be expanded pairwise

For the Brauer class (1.7), bilinearity gives

$$
\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\|_{G_{\mathbf Q}}^2
=\sum_{i,j}n_i n_j
\langle A_{i,\tau},A_{j,\tau}\rangle_{G_{\mathbf Q}}.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Thus effectivity will follow if the finite matrix

$$
G_\tau=(\langle A_{i,\tau},A_{j,\tau}\rangle)_{i,j}
\tag{3.2}
$$

is independent of $\tau$. It is called the **Mackey Gram matrix** of the Brauer datum. The
entries are nonnegative integers, even though the quadratic combination in (3.1) uses signed
coefficients.

The induced representations have large dimensions, so comparing them directly would conceal
the common packet from which they came. Frobenius reciprocity moves one induction across the
Hom sign, and Mackey decomposition breaks the remaining restriction into intersection fields.
That is precisely the geometry on which Book 183 supplied compatible packets.

### 3.2 Induction for finite-index subgroups

We use Book 180's finite-coset induction and both adjunctions:

$$
\operatorname{Ind}_H^GU=L[G]\otimes_{L[H]}U, \tag{3.3}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_H^GU,V)
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_H(U,V|_H), \tag{3.6}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_G(V,\operatorname{Ind}_H^GU)
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_H(V|_H,U). \tag{3.7}
$$

Finite-index restriction and induction preserve semisimplicity in characteristic zero, so the
actual $A_{i,\tau}$ enter the Hom calculation.

### 3.3 Mackey decomposition with conjugation

For $x\in G$, the conjugate is defined by the rule
$({}^xW)(xjx^{-1})=W(j)$. Book 180 gives

$$
\operatorname{Res}_H^G\operatorname{Ind}_J^GW
\simeq\bigoplus_{x\in H\backslash G/J}
\operatorname{Ind}_{H\cap xJx^{-1}}^H
({}^xW)|_{H\cap xJx^{-1}}. \tag{3.10}
$$

All double-coset sets are finite in the present profinite application.

### 3.4 The Hom form of Mackey theory

Combining the two adjunctions with (3.10) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Hom}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_H^GU,\operatorname{Ind}_J^GW)
\simeq\bigoplus_{x\in H\backslash G/J}
\operatorname{Hom}_{H\cap xJx^{-1}}
(U|_{H\cap xJx^{-1}},{}^xW|_{H\cap xJx^{-1}}).
\end{aligned} \tag{3.12}
$$

Hence

$$
\left\langle\operatorname{Ind}_H^GU,\operatorname{Ind}_J^GW\right\rangle
=\sum_x\left\langle U|_{H\cap xJx^{-1}},
{}^xW|_{H\cap xJx^{-1}}\right\rangle. \tag{3.13}
$$

This is the imported formal mechanism. Section 3.5 supplies the application-specific packet
identification on each intersection field.

### 3.5 Translation to intersection fields

Take $G=G_{\mathbf Q}$, $H=G_{F_i}$, and $J=G_{F_j}$. Double cosets are identified with

$$
H_i\backslash\Gamma/H_j.
\tag{3.14}
$$

For $x\in\Gamma$, put

$$
D_{ij}(x)=H_i\cap xH_jx^{-1},
\qquad
L_{ij}(x)=M^{D_{ij}(x)}=F_i\,xF_j.
\tag{3.15}
$$

The equality on the right is Galois correspondence: the compositum of fixed fields corresponds
to the intersection of subgroups. Since $D_{ij}(x)$ is a subgroup of the elementary group
$H_i$, it is elementary. This elementary-intersection fact is the hinge between finite-group
theory and the packet package.

Let

$$
\alpha_{ij,x}=\theta_i|_{D_{ij}(x)},
\qquad
\beta_{ij,x}=({}^x\theta_j)|_{D_{ij}(x)},
\qquad
\delta_{ij,x}=\beta_{ij,x}\alpha_{ij,x}^{-1}.
\tag{3.16}
$$

Book 183, Proposition 6.2 and (6.4e)--(6.4h), identifies both restricted rank-two
representations with $r_{D_{ij}(x),\tau}$ and identifies their twisted Hom space with the
self-twist by $\delta_{ij,x,\tau}$. Inserting that exported Hom-ready identity into (3.13)
gives

$$
\boxed{
\langle A_{i,\tau},A_{j,\tau}\rangle_{G_{\mathbf Q}}
=\sum_{x\in H_i\backslash\Gamma/H_j}
\dim_\Omega\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{L_{ij}(x)}}
\left(r_{D_{ij}(x),\tau},
r_{D_{ij}(x),\tau}\otimes\delta_{ij,x,\tau}\right).}
\tag{3.17}
$$

There is no loss of Hom information in the semisimplification symbols of the overlap theorem.
The groups $G_{L_{ij}(x)}$ have finite index in the two packet Galois groups, and restriction of
a characteristic-zero semisimple representation to a finite-index subgroup is semisimple by
the normal-core and finite-quotient averaging argument. Thus the actual restrictions occurring
in Mackey are the semisimple representations identified by Book 183.

All coefficient, field-conjugation, and twist directions are visible in (3.17). In particular,
the quotient character is $\beta\alpha^{-1}$, not its inverse by guesswork: tensoring both
arguments by $\alpha^{-1}$ fixes the direction.

The imported comparison is applied separately to every representative in
$H_i\backslash\Gamma/H_j$. Distinct double cosets are not merged even when their intersection
fields or packet representations happen to be abstractly isomorphic. The extension
$L_{ij}(x)/F_i$ may be non-Galois; ordinary Galois restriction, Book 183's algebraic
Frobenius comparison, and its Brauer--Nesbitt recognition are what justify the identification.
No automorphic base change along this extension is used. At a general $\tau$ this is a
semisimple characteristic-zero calculation. Choices of overlap intertwiners can change the
displayed vector-space isomorphism, but not its dimension, and no lattice comparison enters the
Gram matrix.

### 3.6 A diagnostic calculation for S3

For

$$
1_{S_3}=\operatorname{Ind}_{C_2}^{S_3}1-
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_3}^{S_3}\omega,
\tag{3.18}
$$

the virtual arithmetic class is a six-dimensional induced representation minus a
four-dimensional one. Its norm is not $6^2-4^2$, nor is it the difference of the two individual
endomorphism dimensions. If the two terms are $A$ and $B$, then

$$
\|[A]-[B]\|^2=\langle A,A\rangle+\langle B,B\rangle
-2\langle A,B\rangle.
\tag{3.19}
$$

The cross term is decisive. Mackey computes $\langle A,B\rangle$ on the trivial intersection
$C_2\cap xC_3x^{-1}=1$, hence over the compositum of the cubic and quadratic fixed fields. The
two packet restrictions are the same selected top-compatible packet there. Formula (3.17) then
asks only whether it has the finite-order self-twist produced by $\omega$. This small example
already contains the general proof.

## 4. Clifford theory and the common top block

Book 180, Chapter 5 is the general source for homogeneous restriction, inertia groups,
projective multiplicity representations, factor sets, and extension torsors. We retain the
notation needed to locate the SP summands in one Clifford block.

### 4.1 Why a normal subgroup organizes the constituents

For $N\triangleleft G$ of finite index and an irreducible $G$-representation $V$, choose an
irreducible constituent $T$ of $V|_N$. If

$$
I_G(T)=\{g\in G:{}^gT\simeq T\}, \tag{4.1}
$$

Book 180 gives the homogeneous restriction

$$
V|_N\simeq e\bigoplus_{g\in G/I_G(T)}{}^gT. \tag{4.2}
$$

Thus irreducibles above distinct $G$-orbits of $N$-constituents occupy orthogonal Clifford
blocks.

### 4.2 The isotypic block when the top representation is invariant

When $T$ is $G$-invariant, $V|_N\simeq T^{\oplus e}$. Intertwiners for the conjugation action
have a scalar factor set

$$
I_gI_h=c(g,h)I_{gh}, \tag{4.4}
$$

and the multiplicity space

$$
P=\operatorname{Hom}_N(T,V) \tag{4.7}
$$

carries the inverse projective multiplier. Book 180 proves that the multipliers cancel on
$T\otimes P$ and that

$$
e=\dim P,\qquad \dim V=e\dim T. \tag{4.8}
$$

No vanishing of the factor set is assumed here; the norm computation does not choose descent
intertwiners.

### 4.3 The block decomposition of a semisimple category

If $N\leq J\leq G$ and $U|_N$ is a sum of conjugates of $T$, then
$\operatorname{Ind}_J^GU$ lies in the block of the $G$-orbit of $T$. Equivalently,

$$
\operatorname{Res}_N^G\operatorname{Ind}_J^GU
\simeq\bigoplus_{g\in G/J}{}^g(U|_N). \tag{4.9}
$$

This structural statement explains why the later Mackey entries reduce to finite quotient
self-twists; their numerical values still require Book 183's exact overlaps and the computation
in Chapters 5--6.

### 4.4 Application to the Brauer summands

Take $N=G_M$, $G=G_{\mathbf Q}$, and

$$
T_\tau=r_{\pi_M,\tau}.
\tag{4.10}
$$

Conjugation compatibility of the top packet makes the isomorphism class of $T_\tau$ invariant
under $G/N=\Gamma$. Since $\theta_i$ factors through $G_{F_i}/G_M=H_i$, it is trivial on
$G_M$. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{G_M}^{G_{\mathbf Q}}A_{i,\tau}
\simeq T_\tau^{\oplus[\Gamma:H_i]}.
\tag{4.11}
$$

Every $A_{i,\tau}$ lies in the single Clifford block above $T_\tau$. Taking the signed sum and
using the rank-one identity obtained from (1.4),

$$
\sum_i n_i[\Gamma:H_i]=1,
\tag{4.12}
$$

gives

$$
\operatorname{Res}_{G_M}^{G_{\mathbf Q}}
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=[T_\tau].
\tag{4.13}
$$

Formula (4.13) is a powerful consistency check, but it is not itself effectivity. In the
projective representation ring of $\Gamma$, an integral combination
$2[P_1]-[P_2]$ can have total projective dimension one. The norm is what rules out such hidden
cancellation.

Clifford theory also explains the characters in (3.16). Two extensions of the same irreducible
top representation to an intermediate group differ, when they differ by a scalar ambiguity,
by a character of the finite quotient. The selected cyclic descents of Book 183 choose one
extension, and its elementary-intersection theorem compares those choices on
$G_{M^{D_{ij}(x)}}$ itself. It thereby removes any additional quotient-character ambiguity
before the Brauer characters are inserted. Hence every overlap Hom space is governed by the one
explicit finite quotient character $\delta_{ij,x}$.

## 5. Elementary overlap packets and their self-twists

### 5.1 The overlap packet really is one packet

Fix $i,j$ and $x\in\Gamma$, and abbreviate

$$
D=H_i\cap xH_jx^{-1},
\qquad L=M^D.
\tag{5.1}
$$

There are two routes from the top packet $\pi_M$ toward $L$: through $F_i$, or through
$xF_j$. The subgroup $D$ need not be normal in either elementary group, so neither route is
silently interpreted as automorphic base change to $L$. Instead Book 183 applies its
Proposition 6.2 twice. Since $D\leq H_i$, it gives

$$
\left(r_{H_i,\tau}|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}
\simeq r_{D,\tau}.
\tag{5.1a}
$$

Since $D\leq xH_jx^{-1}$, the same proposition and conjugation compatibility give

$$
\left({}^x r_{H_j,\tau}|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}
\simeq r_{D,\tau}.
\tag{5.1b}
$$

These identities hold for every coefficient embedding into an algebraically closed
characteristic-zero nonarchimedean field, after placing the finite local fields of definition
in one common finite overfield. They are exactly Book 183's equation (6.4e).

The proof there handles the non-Galois case directly. At a good place of $L$, restriction raises
the two downstairs Frobenius roots by the residue degree. At the distinguished embedding, both
resulting polynomials are the polynomial of the already existing $\rho|_{G_L}$. Injectivity of
the coefficient embedding makes the equality algebraic in $K[X]$, and Book 183's
Chebotarev--Brauer--Nesbitt lemma identifies the two already existing semisimple
representations over $G_L$ at every embedding. Book 184 imports this completed theorem; it does
not add a density theorem to the effectivity argument and does not use any conclusion of Book
185.

After the rank-one twists are restored, (5.1a)--(5.1b) become

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left((r_{H_i,\tau}\otimes\theta_{i,\tau})|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}
&\simeq r_{D,\tau}\otimes\alpha_{ij,x,\tau},\\
\left({}^x(r_{H_j,\tau}\otimes\theta_{j,\tau})|_{G_L}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}
&\simeq r_{D,\tau}\otimes\beta_{ij,x,\tau}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1c}
$$

Thus the only ratio in the Hom calculation is
$\delta_{ij,x}=\beta_{ij,x}\alpha_{ij,x}^{-1}$. A quotient character that relates two
descriptions of the untwisted packet can survive only as a genuine self-twist of
$r_{D,\tau}$ and hence does not change its semisimple isomorphism class. Book 183 neither
declares such a self-twist character trivial nor chooses a coherent intertwiner; neither is
needed for a Hom dimension.

Finally, (5.1a)--(5.1c) are characteristic-zero statements at every embedding. They supply no
lattice, homothety, integral intertwiner, residual comparison, or compatible cocycle. The norm
argument uses only their semisimple characteristic-zero Hom spaces.

### 5.2 Absolute irreducibility of an elementary packet realization

We need Schur's lemma for $r_{D,\tau}$. The imported SP record proves what is needed at the
selected $\nu\mid3$. Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate extends that argument to
every embedding away from residue characteristic two; hypothesis $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ supplies
the separate coefficient-two case.

The order is load-bearing. Book 183 must establish its raw-to-global preservation certificate
before this proposition is invoked. It would be circular to use Proposition 5.1's absolute
irreducibility to justify that preservation: the proof below deduces absolute irreducibility
from the already preserved nonzero monodromy operator. Book 183, Proposition 6.1 supplies two
permissible prior routes, neither using this effectivity argument: the preferred
$(\mathrm{HT}_1)$ route proves packet irreducibility directly by the algebraic-Hecke-character
contradiction, while the stronger ambient route proves semisimplicity of the raw carrier
factor. The first route also supplies $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ for the top packet.

**Proposition 5.1.** Let $D\leq\Gamma$ be elementary. If $\tau$ induces the selected place
$\nu\mid3$, the semisimple representation $r_{D,\tau}$ is absolutely irreducible. Under Book
183's optional uniform packet certificate, the same holds for every $q(\tau)\ne2$. If
$q(\tau)=2$, it holds under $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$.

**Proof.** Suppose first that $\tau$ induces the selected $\nu\mid3$, or that the optional
uniform packet certificate applies and the residue characteristic of $\tau$ is not two. Since
$2$ splits completely in $M$, choose a dyadic place of $F_D$. In precisely these cases Book
183's algebraic SP record identifies the Frobenius-semisimple Weil--Deligne pair of
$r_{D,\tau}$ there and gives it nonzero rank-one monodromy. If the semisimple global
representation were reducible, then over the algebraically closed coefficient field it would
be a sum of two continuous characters,

$$
r_{D,\tau}=\eta_1\oplus\eta_2
\tag{5.2}
$$

whose local Weil--Deligne pair has monodromy zero. This contradicts the imported SP pair.
Hence $r_{D,\tau}$ is absolutely irreducible in this case.

Suppose now that the residue characteristic is two. Proposition 6.2 of Book 183 applied to
$1\leq D$ gives

$$
\left(r_{D,\tau}|_{G_M}\right)^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq r_{1,\tau}.
\tag{5.3}
$$

The right side is absolutely irreducible by $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$. If $r_{D,\tau}$ had an invariant
line, its restriction to $G_M$ would retain that line and its semisimplification would be
reducible, contradicting (5.3). Thus $r_{D,\tau}$ is absolutely irreducible. $\square$

No coefficient-place de Rham or Hodge--Tate assertion is used. In particular, cuspidality and
good Frobenius polynomials alone have not been treated as a proof of Galois irreducibility.
A coefficient-prime comparison identifying the two-adic local realization with the signed SP
object would be stronger: its nonzero monodromy would rule out a decomposition into two
characters, because every one-dimensional semistable Weil--Deligne object has zero monodromy.
It would therefore imply $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ and would also supply a two-adic SP record that
$(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ by itself does not contain. Such a comparison lies outside the scope of the
selected-three main theorem, which uses the SP object only at a coefficient place above three.

For the optional coefficient-two extension, the argument needs only the irreducibility in
$(\mathrm{AI}_2)$, or one of the finite Gram conditions stated in Chapter 1. Neither
cyclotomic determinant nor parity can replace it: the reducible semisimple representation
$1\oplus\chi_2$ has both the cyclotomic determinant and the required eigenvalues of complex
conjugation. Likewise, common Frobenius polynomials do not transport monodromy or an invariant
line between coefficient fields of different residue characteristic. The optional local
comparison and the norm-one effectivity argument thus have different conclusions, and only the
latter is needed here.

### 5.3 Self-twist groups

Fix an elementary $D\leq\Gamma$ and put $F=F_D$ and $\pi_F=\pi_D$.
Let $\Delta$ be a finite group quotient of $G_F$ through which the finite characters under
consideration factor. For a finite-order character $\delta:G_F\to K^\times$, let
$\psi_\delta$ be its finite-order Hecke avatar. To shorten notation, write
$\pi_F\otimes\delta$ for the automorphic twist
$\pi_F\otimes(\psi_\delta\circ\det)$ and define

$$
\operatorname{ST}_F(\pi_F)=
\{\delta:\pi_F\otimes\delta\simeq\pi_F\}.
\tag{5.4}
$$

Only finitely many $\delta$ occur in this book, namely the ratios (3.16), so no assertion about
the size of the full group of all finite-order characters is needed.

**Proposition 5.2 (embedding-independent self-twist test).** Let $\tau$ be a coefficient
embedding for which $r_{\pi_F,\tau}$ is absolutely irreducible. For every such $\delta$,

$$
\dim_\Omega\operatorname{Hom}_{G_F}
\bigl(r_{\pi_F,\tau},r_{\pi_F,\tau}\otimes\delta_\tau\bigr)
{}={}
\begin{cases}
1,&\delta\in\operatorname{ST}_F(\pi_F),\\
0,&\delta\notin\operatorname{ST}_F(\pi_F).
\end{cases}
\tag{5.5}
$$

In particular the dimension has the displayed value at the selected $\nu\mid3$. Under Book
183's optional uniform packet certificate it has that coefficient-independent value throughout
the away-from-two range; together with $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ this gives the all-coefficient range.

**Proof.** Absolute irreducibility and Schur's lemma show that the Hom space has dimension one
if the two representations are isomorphic and zero otherwise. Proposition 5.1 supplies this
irreducibility in the ranges asserted after the formula.

If $\pi_F\otimes\delta\simeq\pi_F$, compatibility of twisting with Galois realization gives

$$
r_{\pi_F,\tau}\otimes\delta_\tau\simeq r_{\pi_F,\tau}
\tag{5.6}
$$

for every $\tau$. Conversely, suppose (5.6) holds for one $\tau$. At every unramified place
$w$ it gives equality of the two characteristic polynomials after applying $\tau$. Their
coefficients are algebraic and $\tau$ is injective, so the polynomials were already equal in
the common number field. Hence the local Hecke parameters of $\pi_F$ and
$\pi_F\otimes\delta$ agree at almost every place. Strong multiplicity one gives
$\pi_F\otimes\delta\simeq\pi_F$. The criterion is therefore automorphic and independent of the
coefficient embedding. $\square$

There is a useful trace shadow of the same test. A self-twist necessarily gives, at every good
$w$,

$$
a_w(\pi_F)\delta(\operatorname{Frob}_w)=a_w(\pi_F).
\tag{5.7}
$$

Together with equality of determinants, the trace identity at almost every $w$ gives equality
of the unramified local polynomials, and strong multiplicity one then gives the self-twist. Trace
equality alone must not be substituted for the determinant condition. If (5.7) fails at one good
place, its nonzero algebraic difference remains nonzero under every field embedding. This makes
the embedding independence visible at the coefficient level.

### 5.4 Determinants constrain, but do not determine, self-twists

Since

$$
\det(r_{\pi_F,\tau}\otimes\delta_\tau)
=\det(r_{\pi_F,\tau})\delta_\tau^2,
\tag{5.8}
$$

every self-twist satisfies $\delta^2=1$. Thus only quadratic characters can stabilize a
rank-two realization with fixed determinant. The converse is false: a quadratic character need
not be a self-twist. Equality in (5.8) is only a necessary determinant condition, while (5.5)
tests the full representation.

The equation $\delta_\tau^2=1$ at one embedding is an algebraic equation among roots of unity;
injectivity of $\tau$ gives $\delta^2=1$ before embedding. This determinant check is therefore
coefficient independent, but it is not used as a substitute for the self-twist test.

This distinction prevents a shortcut that would be fatal in the norm calculation. Replacing
each Hom dimension by the indicator of $\delta^2=1$ would overcount quadratic characters that
do not stabilize the packet.

### 5.5 Continuity and coefficient placement of the twists

Every $\delta$ in (3.16) factors through the finite group $D$ and has root-of-unity values.
After the common enlargement of $K$, its avatar $\delta_\tau$ is defined for every $\tau$, is
continuous, and preserves a free rank-one lattice because all its values are units. Field
conjugation changes $D$ and transports $\delta$; coefficient conjugation applies an automorphism
to its root-of-unity values. Proposition 5.2 is compatible with both operations, since they
transport an isomorphism of packets to an isomorphism of the transported packets.

## 6. Independence of the Mackey Gram matrix

### 6.1 The entrywise calculation

For a double coset $x\in H_i\backslash\Gamma/H_j$, define

$$
\varepsilon_{ij}(x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\delta_{ij,x}\in
\operatorname{ST}_{L_{ij}(x)}(\pi_{D_{ij}(x)}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.1}
$$

At the selected $\nu\mid3$, and under Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate for every
$q(\tau)\ne2$, Proposition 5.2 turns (3.17) into the entirely coefficient-free formula

$$
\boxed{
\langle A_{i,\tau},A_{j,\tau}\rangle_{G_{\mathbf Q}}
=\sum_{x\in H_i\backslash\Gamma/H_j}\varepsilon_{ij}(x).}
\tag{6.2}
$$

The right side depends on the finite subgroup geometry, the selected overlap packets, and their
finite self-twist groups. It does not depend on $\tau$.

When $q(\tau)=2$, $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ implies equation (6.2) term by term for the
finite list of triples belonging to this Brauer datum. Hypothesis $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ proves that
condition through Propositions 5.1--5.2. Without either input,
(3.17) remains valid, but a reducible overlap representation can have an endomorphism space of
dimension greater than one or a rank-one Hom which is not an isomorphism. The automorphic
self-twist indicator then does not determine the Hom dimension.

The formula also passes a symmetry check. Reversing $(i,j)$ sends a double coset represented by
$x$ to the one represented by $x^{-1}$, transports the intersection packet, and replaces the
character ratio by its inverse. A self-twist group is closed under inversion, so the indicator
is unchanged, as required by the symmetry of the Hom pairing.

**Theorem 6.1 (Gram invariance in its exact range).** At the selected embedding inducing
$\nu\mid3$, the Mackey Gram matrix $G_\tau$ of (3.2) is the same integral matrix as at the
distinguished embedding. Under Book 183's optional uniform packet certificate, that matrix is
the same for every coefficient embedding of residue characteristic different from two. At a
coefficient-two embedding it is that same matrix under $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$.
Consequently the optional uniform certificate together with $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ makes the matrix
independent of $\tau$ over all coefficient embeddings.

**Proof.** Formula (6.2) proves entrywise equality at the selected embedding and, under the
optional uniform packet certificate, in the away-from-two range. Book 183's
overlap theorem applies to every one of the finitely many double cosets and places both Hom
arguments over the same $r_{D_{ij}(x),\tau}$. Every double-coset set and every algebraic
character $\delta_{ij,x}=\beta_{ij,x}\alpha_{ij,x}^{-1}$ is fixed before a coefficient
embedding is chosen. Proposition 5.2 proves that each resulting self-twist indicator is
unchanged in that range. At residue characteristic two, $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ asserts
the same entrywise formula, and $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ implies that assertion. $\square$

### 6.2 Why all intersections, not just the top field, are needed

It is tempting to restrict both induced representations directly to $G_M$ and use (4.11).
That loses information. All summands then become copies of $T_\tau$, so restriction sees only
the product $[\Gamma:H_i][\Gamma:H_j]$; it forgets which coset intertwiners descend to
$G_{\mathbf Q}$. The double-coset intersections in (6.2) retain precisely that descent
information.

Equivalently, $\operatorname{Hom}_{G_M}$ counts every top-field intertwiner, while
$\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{\mathbf Q}}$ counts only those satisfying the quotient compatibility.
The self-twist indicators $\varepsilon_{ij}(x)$ impose that compatibility one orbit at a time.

### 6.3 Norm invariance

Let $n=(n_1,\ldots,n_t)^T$. Equations (3.1) and (3.2) give

$$
\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\|^2=n^TG_\tau n.
\tag{6.3}
$$

Theorem 6.1 therefore proves:

**Corollary 6.2.** The nonnegative integer
$\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\|^2$ has the distinguished value at the selected
$\nu\mid3$. Under the optional uniform packet certificate it is independent of $\tau$ away
from residue characteristic two. It has the same value at a coefficient-two embedding under
$(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$.

The signs $n_i$ cause no difficulty. They enter only after the positive semidefinite Gram matrix
has been computed. In particular, there is no claim that each term of the signed quadratic sum
is nonnegative.

### 6.4 Evaluation at the distinguished embedding

At $\tau_0$, Book 183's tensor identity gives

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau_0}=[\rho\otimes_E\Omega].
\tag{6.4}
$$

Absolute irreducibility of $\rho$ is part of the controlled input (1.1). Schur's lemma yields

$$
\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau_0}\|^2
=\dim_\Omega\operatorname{End}_{G_{\mathbf Q}}(\rho\otimes_E\Omega)=1.
\tag{6.5}
$$

Combining (6.5) with Corollary 6.2 gives the decisive equality

$$
\boxed{\|\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\|^2=1
\quad\text{if }\tau\text{ induces }\nu\mid3,
\text{ if the optional uniform certificate applies and }q(\tau)\ne2,
\text{ or if }q(\tau)=2\text{ and }(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))\text{ holds}.}
\tag{6.6}
$$

At a coefficient-two embedding, $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ is precisely equation (6.6)
without the stronger assertion that every Gram entry agrees with its away-from-two value.
Thus

$$
(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))
\Longrightarrow(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B)),
\tag{6.6a}
$$

and no converse is required. This proves the complete implication from the finite Hom gate to
the minimal numerical gate using only the imported overlaps and the distinguished norm-one
calculation.

This is the only propagation from the distinguished member. We propagate an integer Gram
matrix whose entries have separately been shown embedding independent. We do not claim that an
isomorphism or matrix model at $\tau_0$ can simply be transported through an unrelated
nonarchimedean completion.

### 6.5 Logical order and circularity

The proof of (6.6) does not assume effectivity at a new embedding. Every $A_{i,\tau}$ is an
actual induced representation before signs are introduced. Frobenius reciprocity and Mackey
apply to these actual terms. Proposition 5.1 concerns the already existing elementary-field
cuspidal realizations, not the desired base-field representation. Proposition 5.2 uses
automorphic twisting and strong multiplicity one over an elementary field, again before any
base-field object is constructed. Finally, norm one is evaluated at $\tau_0$ using the
pre-existing $\rho$ from the controlled datum. No conclusion of this book has been used as an input.

Nor is Book 183's proof of the overlap theorem circular here. Its
Chebotarev--Brauer--Nesbitt step compares two packet representations that already exist over the
elementary intersection field; it does not construct or assume the effective base-field
representation of Theorem 7.1. The overlap is characteristic zero, so the Gram calculation
does not assume an integral cancellation. Coherent coefficient-family assembly is absent from
both the overlap input and the norm proof.

The same logical check applies to local preservation. The nonzero $N$ used in Proposition 5.1 is an
input from Book 183, not an output reconstructed from the effective constituent. Neither the
Gram norm nor Theorem 7.1 may be run backward to prove that the raw packet pair survived global
semisimplification.

## 7. Positivity and the effective representation

### 7.1 The integral multiplicity argument

Fix $\tau$ for which (6.6) is known. This includes the selected $\nu\mid3$ and, under the
optional uniform packet certificate, every away-from-two embedding; at a coefficient-two
embedding it is exactly the hypothesis $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$. Decompose
the virtual semisimple class into irreducibles:

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}
=\sum_{a=1}^s m_a[S_a],
\qquad m_a\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{7.1}
$$

The coefficients are integers because every Brauer coefficient $n_i$ is integral and each
$A_{i,\tau}$ has integral Jordan--Hölder multiplicities. Equation (6.6) says

$$
\sum_{a=1}^s m_a^2=1.
\tag{7.2}
$$

Thus exactly one $m_a$ is $1$ or $-1$. On the other hand, Book 183's rank calculation gives

$$
2=\dim\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}
=\sum_a m_a\dim S_a.
\tag{7.3}
$$

The negative sign would make the right side negative. Hence the unique coefficient is $+1$ and
the corresponding irreducible has dimension two.

**Theorem 7.1 (positivity and the exact norm gate).** Suppose that $\tau$ induces the selected
$\nu\mid3$, or that the optional uniform packet certificate applies and $q(\tau)\ne2$, or that
$q(\tau)=2$ and $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ holds. Then there is a unique isomorphism class of
absolutely irreducible two-dimensional semisimple representation $r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ such
that

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=[r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}].
\tag{7.4}
$$

Conversely, if $q(\tau)=2$ and the class is represented by an absolutely irreducible
rank-two representation, then $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ holds.

**Proof.** At the selected $\nu\mid3$, and throughout the away-from-two range under the
optional uniform packet certificate, the application-specific work is (6.2)--(6.6): every Gram
entry is reduced to a packet self-twist and compared with the distinguished one, whose norm is
one. Under $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ the same proof works at residue
characteristic two. Under the weaker $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$, equation (7.2) is the
hypothesis itself. In every case Book 180, Theorem 7.2 applies to (7.2)--(7.3), giving the
positive irreducible rank-two constituent and the cancellation (7.5). Uniqueness follows from
irreducible multiplicities. Conversely, the class of one absolutely irreducible representation
has character norm one by Schur's lemma. $\square$

This proves positive sign, dimension two, norm one, and irreducibility at every coefficient
embedding in the range of Theorem 7.1. The four assertions are logically distinct: norm one
gives one signed irreducible;
positive dimension fixes the sign; the rank calculation fixes its dimension. Integrality of the
Brauer coefficients is what makes the signed irreducible coefficient an integer. Neither the
cyclotomic determinant, purity, nor the quadratic Frobenius polynomial is used to choose the
sign. Since $\Omega$ is algebraically closed, irreducibility of the surviving constituent is
absolute irreducibility.

### 7.2 Cancellation as an actual characteristic-zero isomorphism

Return to (1.11). Since all terms are semisimple, equality (7.4) of Grothendieck classes means
that their irreducible multiplicities satisfy

$$
R_\tau^+\simeq r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\oplus R_\tau^-.
\tag{7.5}
$$

This is an actual isomorphism of characteristic-zero $G_{\mathbf Q}$-representations. It is
obtained by choosing decompositions into irreducibles and matching equal constituents, so it is
not canonical. Adding the same representation to both $R_\tau^+$ and $R_\tau^-$ changes the
presentation and the possible matching maps but not $r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$.

Equation (7.5) is the precise meaning of cancellation. It should not be lifted without proof to
an isomorphism of selected integral lattices. A characteristic-zero irreducible summand can meet
an ambient lattice in a nonsaturated submodule, and primes dividing $|\Gamma|$ can introduce
extensions after reduction.

This is semisimple effectivity, not an assertion about an arbitrary nonsplit representative of
the same character. The semisimple Grothendieck group records exactly the irreducible
multiplicities matched in (7.5); it forgets extension classes, and the norm argument does not
recover them.

### 7.3 The determinant becomes an actual determinant

The determinant is multiplicative in the semisimple Grothendieck group. Book 183 proved the
virtual identity

$$
\det\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=\chi_\tau,
\tag{7.6}
$$

including the transfer of $\theta_i^2$, the permutation signs, and the rank identity from the
Brauer relation. Substituting (7.4) gives

$$
\boxed{\det r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}=\chi_\tau.}
\tag{7.7}
$$

No choice of a square root or determinant-normalizing twist is made at this stage. All twists
have already canceled in the determinant-line calculation. In particular, replacing the
actual determinant by a post hoc choice would destroy the coefficient and local compatibility
that (7.7) inherits.

The two sign mechanisms remain separate: rank two squares away each coset-permutation sign in
an induced packet, while the determinant of the one-dimensional Brauer relation cancels the
transferred character squares only after the signed product over all terms. Effectivity adds no
new determinant computation; it identifies the already proved virtual line with the determinant
of the actual constituent.

### 7.4 The distinguished member

At $\tau_0$, equations (6.4) and (7.4) give

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau_0}\simeq\rho\otimes_E\Omega.
\tag{7.8}
$$

This identification is representation theoretic and hence respects every global restriction.
In particular it recovers the SP pair (1.1a)--(1.1c) at $2$. There is no named lattice or
integral quotient system to recover, and none is reconstructed by the norm argument.

## 8. Coefficient fields, continuity, and lattices

### 8.1 Continuity of the effective constituent

Every $A_{i,\tau}$ is continuous. Therefore $R_\tau^+$ is a continuous representation on a
finite-dimensional $\Omega$-space. By (7.5), $r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ is a $G_{\mathbf Q}$-stable
direct summand. A linear subspace of a finite-dimensional nonarchimedean vector space is closed,
and the restricted action map

$$
G_{\mathbf Q}\times r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\longrightarrow r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}
\tag{8.1}
$$

is the restriction of the continuous action on $R_\tau^+$. Hence
$r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ is continuous.

This proof uses actual cancellation (7.5), not merely continuity of the good-prime trace
function. A continuous class function on a dense set does not by itself provide matrices or an
action law.

### 8.2 A finite local coefficient field

Although $\tau$ takes values in an algebraic closure, the effective representation has a model
over a finite extension of the completion $K_\tau$. To see this, first put every induced
summand over one finite extension $L/K_\tau$ containing the packet realizations and the values
of the finite characters, and write the resulting $L$-models as $R_{\tau,L}^\pm$. Let $B$ be
the image of the group algebra $L[G_{\mathbf Q}]$ in

$$
\operatorname{End}_L(R_{\tau,L}^+\oplus R_{\tau,L}^-).
\tag{8.2}
$$

It is a finite-dimensional $L$-algebra because it is a subspace of a finite-dimensional matrix
algebra. After a finite extension $L'/L$ chosen inside $\Omega$, its semisimple quotient splits:
the finitely many
simple factors acquire matrix models

$$
(B/\operatorname{rad}B)\otimes_LL'
\simeq\prod_a M_{d_a}(L').
$$

The absolutely irreducible $\Omega$-constituent singled out by (7.4) corresponds to one of
these factors. Projecting the group-algebra action to that factor and taking its standard simple
column module gives an $L'$-representation whose scalar extension is
$r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$. The original action into $B^\times$ is continuous, and the quotient,
scalar-extension, factor-projection, and standard matrix action are continuous maps between
finite-dimensional nonarchimedean spaces. Thus this $L'$-model is continuous. Its dimension is
two by Theorem 7.1, so it supplies

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau,L'}:G_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(L')
\tag{8.3}
$$

whose scalar extension to $\Omega$ is (7.4). The central idempotent belonging to one simple
factor would select the full isotypic factor, possibly with multiplicity; the standard simple
module of the split matrix factor is what selects one copy. No idempotent is asserted to
preserve any chosen integral lattice.

This is a finite field-of-definition statement at one coefficient place. It does not say that
the same number field, without completion, contains matrices for all coefficient places. The
obstruction to such simultaneous descent includes Schur indices and coefficient cocycles and is
part of Book 185's assembly problem.

### 8.3 Existence of a stable lattice

Let $L'$ be as in (8.3), with ring of integers $\mathcal O_{L'}$. The compact image of the
profinite group $G_{\mathbf Q}$ preserves some lattice.

**Lemma 8.1 (stable lattice).** Every continuous representation of a profinite group on a
finite-dimensional vector space over a finite extension of $\mathbf Q_q$ has a stable
$\mathcal O_{L'}$-lattice.

**Proof.** Start with any lattice $\Lambda_0$. Its stabilizer in
$\operatorname{GL}_2(L')$ is an open subgroup. The compact image of $G_{\mathbf Q}$ therefore
has only finitely many translates of $\Lambda_0$. The sum of those finitely many lattices,

$$
\Lambda=\sum_{g\in G_{\mathbf Q}}r(g)\Lambda_0,
\tag{8.4}
$$

where the sum means the finite set of distinct translates, is again a lattice and is stable.
$\square$

Thus effectivity supplies at least one rank-two stable lattice. It supplies neither a preferred
lattice nor a comparison with lattices chosen separately on the positive and negative induced
terms.
If two stable lattices have absolutely irreducible residual reductions, the usual homothety
argument may identify them up to scale; no such residual irreducibility has been proved here at
an arbitrary coefficient place.

### 8.4 What happens after reduction

Choose a stable lattice $\Lambda_\tau$ and reduce modulo its maximal ideal. The semisimplified
residual representation

$$
\overline r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}^{\mathrm{ss}}
\tag{8.5}
$$

is defined after the choice of $\Lambda_\tau$. No lattice-independent residual object is needed
or claimed in this book. In particular, the norm proof supplies no comparison between reductions
of two different stable lattices, and the nonsplit residual extension class can depend on that
choice. Any later use of a lattice-independent semisimplified reduction must invoke its own
modular lattice-comparison theorem in the precise coefficient setting where it is needed.

More importantly, (7.5) need not reduce to

$$
\overline R_\tau^+\simeq
\overline r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\oplus\overline R_\tau^-.
\tag{8.6}
$$

The characteristic-zero matching can use denominators, intersections with lattices can be
nonsaturated, and modular representations of the finite quotient need not be semisimple. Thus
the proof of effectivity does not turn Book 183's integral-coefficient signed class into a
literal integral quotient.

At $\tau_0$ the rational representation is the specified $\rho$, but no lattice was part of
the packet-array input. At every coefficient place, any assertion of finite-flatness,
crystalline integral structure, or a particular residual extension requires additional local
integral work.

### 8.5 Coefficient conjugation

Let $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$. Applying $\sigma$ to the Brauer characters and
packet eigenvalues gives the conjugate datum $\mathfrak B^\sigma$. If $\tau$ is a coefficient
embedding, then the induced summands for $(\mathfrak B^\sigma,\tau)$ have the same algebraic
local polynomials as those for $(\mathfrak B,\tau\circ\sigma)$.

The self-twist indicators in (6.1) are also transported by $\sigma$. Whenever $\tau$ is the
selected embedding, or the optional uniform packet certificate or the relevant coefficient-two
gate applies, both virtual classes are therefore effective. At every good $p$ their traces are

$$
\tau(\sigma(a_p)).
\tag{8.7}
$$

The Brauer--Nesbitt theorem proved in Chapter 10 will identify their scalar extensions. For now
we record the exact covariance: coefficient conjugation changes eigenvalues and roots of unity;
it does not move the number fields $F_H$. Field conjugation moves $F_H$ and local places but
does not apply $\sigma$ to an eigenvalue. The two operations commute because the packet ledger
of Book 183 makes them commute before induction.

## 9. Frobenius polynomials and local records after effectivity

### 9.1 The common quadratic polynomial is now effective

Let $p\notin S_0$ be distinct from the residue characteristic of $\tau$. Substituting
(7.4) into Book 183's virtual good-prime identity gives

$$
\det(1-r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}(\operatorname{Frob}_p)T)
=1-\tau(a_p)T+pT^2.
\tag{9.1}
$$

Equivalently, in the characteristic-polynomial variable,

$$
P_{p,\tau}(X)=X^2-\tau(a_p)X+p.
\tag{9.2}
$$

This is stronger than the polynomiality statement of Book 183. There the right side was the
ratio of determinants of two actual representations and was proved to cancel as a rational
function. Here it is the characteristic polynomial of one actual rank-two representation.

The roots retain the purity record proved in Book 183: under every complex embedding they have
absolute value $p^{1/2}$. Purity did not prove effectivity, but after effectivity it becomes the
usual purity of the actual Frobenius eigenvalues.

### 9.2 Local restriction and exact cancellation

The global semisimple isomorphism (7.5) remains an isomorphism after restriction to a
decomposition group:

$$
R_\tau^+|_{G_{\mathbf Q_v}}
\simeq
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}|_{G_{\mathbf Q_v}}
\oplus R_\tau^-|_{G_{\mathbf Q_v}}.
\tag{9.3}
$$

Thus the actual local representation has the local virtual class computed by Mackey:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}|_{G_{\mathbf Q_v}}]
=\sum_i n_i\sum_{w\mid v}
\Bigl[
\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{(F_i)_w}}^{G_{\mathbf Q_v}}
(&r_{H_i,\tau}|_{G_{(F_i)_w}}\\
&\otimes\theta_{i,\tau}|_{G_{(F_i)_w}})
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.4}
$$

Here (9.4) is an equality in the split Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional continuous
local representations. The stronger statement is (9.3), an actual direct-sum isomorphism, so no
semisimplification of the restricted rank-two member is being used to manufacture local
extension data.

Two kinds of Mackey intersection must remain distinct. The Gram matrix used the global
elementary groups $H_i\cap xH_jx^{-1}$ and therefore invoked Book 183's packet comparison over
$M^{H_i\cap xH_jx^{-1}}$. Formula (9.4) uses intersections with a local decomposition group;
those intersections are subgroups of conjugates of the elementary $H_i$ and hence are
elementary, but no packet indexed by them is needed or asserted in the local formula. That
formula follows directly from profinite Mackey applied to the existing summands. When an
elementary-overlap comparison is subsequently restricted to a place of its intersection field,
Book 183's global semisimple isomorphism identifies the corresponding local Galois, and away
from coefficient characteristic Weil--Deligne, isomorphism classes. At a general coefficient
embedding it still gives no integral local intertwiner.

Unlike a bare equality of local characters, (9.3) is inherited from an isomorphism of global
representations. It therefore retains the actual local action, including any extension data
present upon restriction. The matching in (9.3) is noncanonical, but the isomorphism class of
the remaining rank-two summand is canonical.

When $v$ differs from the residue characteristic of $\tau$, apply the Weil--Deligne functor to
(9.3). It commutes with direct sums, restriction, finite induction, and finite-order twists, so

$$
[\operatorname{WD}_v(r_{\mathfrak B,\tau})]
$$

is the effective rank-two class represented by Book 183's virtual Weil--Deligne Mackey sum.
Both the Weil action and the monodromy operator are retained. We do not infer monodromy from
the good Frobenius trace.

### 9.3 The SP local record

The prime $2$ splits completely in $M$. Hence every completion $(F_i)_w$ above $2$ is
$\mathbf Q_2$, every local quotient character $\theta_i$ is trivial there, and every selected
packet has the same transported automorphic local component. Suppose first that the residue
characteristic of $\tau$ is not two. Local--global compatibility identifies all corresponding
Weil--Deligne representations with

$$
\mathscr D_{2,\tau}=\mathscr D_{2,K}\otimes_{K,\tau}\Omega,
$$

where $\mathscr D_{2,K}$ is Book 183's algebraic SP pair. Put

$$
a=\sum_i n_i^+[\Gamma:H_i],
\qquad
b=\sum_i n_i^-[\Gamma:H_i].
$$

Local Mackey gives $a$ copies of $\mathscr D_{2,\tau}$ on the positive side and $b$ copies on
the negative side. Applying Weil--Deligne to the actual direct-sum cancellation (9.3) gives

$$
\mathscr D_{2,\tau}^{\oplus a}
\simeq
\operatorname{WD}_2(r_{\mathfrak B,\tau})^{\mathrm{F\!-\!ss}}
\oplus\mathscr D_{2,\tau}^{\oplus b}.
\tag{9.5}
$$

The rank identity says $a-b=1$. The finite-length Krull--Schmidt cancellation theorem applies
to Weil--Deligne modules: if $U\oplus C\simeq W\oplus C$, comparison of indecomposable
summands gives $U\simeq W$. Indeed, on the direct sum of the finitely many objects at issue,
the algebra generated by finite inertia, Frobenius and its inverse, and $N$ is a
finite-dimensional algebra. Direct-sum decompositions of the pairs are exactly decompositions
as modules for this algebra, where the usual finite-length Krull--Schmidt theorem applies.
Canceling $b$ copies in (9.5) therefore gives the full-pair identity

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{WD}_2(r_{\mathfrak B,\tau})^{\mathrm{F\!-\!ss}}
\simeq\mathscr D_{2,\tau}.}
\tag{9.6}
$$

This is not merely equality of conductor or inertial type. Write the pair as
$(r_{2,\tau},N)$. Then

$$
N^2=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}N=1,
\qquad
\ker N=\operatorname{im}N=\mathscr L_{2,\tau}.
\tag{9.7}
$$

Finite Weil inertia is trivial, and arithmetic Frobenius satisfies

$$
r_{2,\tau}(F_2)Nr_{2,\tau}(F_2)^{-1}=2N.
\tag{9.8}
$$

It acts by $\varepsilon_2$ on the quotient and by $2\varepsilon_2$ on
$\mathscr L_{2,\tau}$. Thus the invariant line and the split/nonsplit sign survive
cancellation. In the corresponding $\tau$-adic representation, inertia invariants are exactly
$\ker N=\mathscr L_{2,\tau}$. The Swan conductor is zero, and the Weil--Deligne conductor
formula gives

$$
a_2(r_{\mathfrak B,\tau})
=\dim V-\dim(\ker N)^{I_2}
=2-1=1.
\tag{9.9}
$$

Conductor one is therefore a consequence of the retained SP pair, not the datum used to
recognize it. When the coefficient characteristic is two, the prime-to-coefficient
Weil--Deligne argument is unavailable. Effectivity still supplies an actual two-adic local
restriction and the exact Mackey expression (9.4); no two-adic SP or integral conclusion is
inferred here.

### 9.4 The coefficient prime

At the distinguished place $\tau_0\mid\ell$, equation (7.8) identifies the representation with
$\rho$ as a rational representation. This retains whatever rational coefficient-prime record
was part of $\rho$, but Book 183 supplied no named lattice, finite-flat quotient system, or
integral projector. The determinant calculation remains a characteristic-zero calculation.

For a new $\tau$ of residue characteristic $q$, effectivity proves that
$r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ is an actual rank-two $q$-adic representation. It does not prove that an
arbitrary stable lattice from Lemma 8.1 is finite flat at $q$. Nor does it prove a clean
crystalline type if $q$ ramifies in $M$ and the quotient characters are ramified. The exact
output is the actual local representation together with the direct-sum identity (9.3). Any
$p$-adic Hodge property supplied for all positive summands and stable under direct summands
passes to this representation; no property is inferred merely by subtracting numerical records
in (9.4). In particular, no finite-flat lattice, compatible quotient tower, or fixed integral
component is obtained at a new coefficient place without separate input.

### 9.5 Archimedean parameters and conductors

Book 183 also exports archimedean and conductor records. Effectivity turns both from signed
invariants into invariants of the actual representation.

Every fixed field $F_i$ is totally real, and its completions at infinity are $\mathbf R$.
The quotient characters $\theta_i$ are trivial on the real decomposition groups. Thus the
restriction of every induced summand to $G_{\mathbf R}$ is a direct sum of
$[\Gamma:H_i]$ copies of the weight-two odd block

$$
1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}.
\tag{9.10}
$$

Using $\sum_i n_i[\Gamma:H_i]=1$ and the actual local cancellation (9.3) gives

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}|_{G_{\mathbf R}}
\simeq1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}.
\tag{9.11}
$$

In particular complex conjugation has eigenvalues $1,-1$ and determinant $-1$, agreeing with
the cyclotomic determinant. The virtual gamma factor $\Gamma_{\mathbf C}(s)$ from Book 183 is
therefore the archimedean factor of the actual effective member, in the same arithmetic
normalization as its finite Euler polynomials.

Now let $v$ be finite and different from the coefficient residue characteristic. Artin
conductors are additive on direct sums. Applying the conductor to (9.3) and then the local
induction formula exported by Book 183 gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_v(r_{\mathfrak B,\tau})
=\sum_i n_i\sum_{w\mid v}
a_{\mathbf Q_v}\left(
\operatorname{Ind}_{(F_i)_w/\mathbf Q_v}
(r_{H_i,\tau,w}\otimes\theta_{i,\tau,w})
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.12}
$$

For one local extension $L/\mathbf Q_v$, write $f(L/\mathbf Q_v)$ for its residue degree and
$d(L/\mathbf Q_v)$ for the valuation of its discriminant ideal, normalized on
$\mathbf Q_v$. Its summand can be calculated as

$$
a_{\mathbf Q_v}(\operatorname{Ind}_{L/\mathbf Q_v}u)
=\dim(u)\,d(L/\mathbf Q_v)+f(L/\mathbf Q_v)a_L(u).
\tag{9.13}
$$

Before effectivity the right side of (9.12) was only a signed integer. It is now the conductor
of an actual rank-two representation and is consequently nonnegative. This does not mean that
the negative terms cancel conductor term by term, nor does it provide a conductor formula for
a residual lattice. At the coefficient prime one may retain rational $p$-adic Hodge records
that are separately supplied and stable under direct summands; integral records require their
own proof and do not follow from the prime-to-coefficient Artin-conductor formalism.

### 9.6 The common bad set

Retain Book 183's finite set $S_0$, containing $2$, the distinguished prime $\ell$, every
rational prime ramified in $M$, every prime below a ramified packet component, and the primes
required by the coefficient and type ledgers. For $\tau$ of residue characteristic $q$, set

$$
S_\tau=S_0\cup\{q\}.
\tag{9.14}
$$

The effective representation is unramified outside $S_\tau$. Indeed each $A_{i,\tau}$ is
unramified there, so both $R_\tau^+$ and its direct summand $r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ are
unramified. At primes in $S_\tau$, (9.4) rather than (9.1) is the controlling record.

Auxiliary ramification may cancel from the effective summand. Effectivity makes that statement
meaningful: if inertia acts trivially on the characteristic-zero constituent, the resulting
rank-two representation is unramified even when the positive and negative presentations contain
ramified terms. No corresponding automatic cancellation is asserted for chosen integral
lattices or residual extensions.

## 10. Brauer–Nesbitt recognition

Book 180, Theorem 8.1 is the recognition theorem used here. It is cited only after Chapters
6--7 have produced actual semisimple representations.

### 10.1 Why traces become decisive only after existence

Trace is unchanged by semisimplification:

$$
\operatorname{tr}_V(g)=\operatorname{tr}_{V^{\mathrm{ss}}}(g). \tag{10.1}
$$

Thus trace data never recover a nonsplit extension, and a virtual Euler product does not become
a representation merely because its coefficients look effective.

### 10.2 The algebra generated by the group

The proof in Book 180 passes to the finite-dimensional image of the group algebra in
$\operatorname{End}(V\oplus W)$ and reads simple multiplicities from the central idempotents of
its semisimple quotient. This applies to arbitrary groups; finiteness of the Galois group image
is not assumed.

### 10.3 The Brauer–Nesbitt theorem

**Theorem 10.1 (Brauer--Nesbitt; Book 180, Theorem 8.1).** If two finite-dimensional
characteristic-zero representations satisfy

$$
\operatorname{tr}_V(g)=\operatorname{tr}_W(g)\qquad(g\in G), \tag{10.6}
$$

then

$$
V^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq W^{\mathrm{ss}}. \tag{10.7}
$$

In rank two, trace and determinant give

$$
\det(X-r(g))=X^2-\operatorname{tr}(r(g))X+\det r(g). \tag{10.8}
$$

### 10.4 The virtual form

Applying Theorem 10.1 to positive-minus-negative presentations gives: two integral semisimple
virtual classes with the same generalized character are equal. This is recognition inside the
Grothendieck group, not an effectivity theorem. Book 183 uses the same principle only to compare
already existing packet restrictions.

### 10.5 Density and continuity

For continuous representations of a profinite group, equality of traces on a dense union of
conjugacy classes extends by continuity, and Theorem 10.1 gives

$$
V^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq W^{\mathrm{ss}}. \tag{10.11}
$$

In the present application the required Frobenius density has already been discharged by Book
183's overlap theorem. No density argument is used to create the effective constituent or to
prove norm one.

## 11. Independence statements proved at the effectivity stage

### 11.1 Independence of a positive-minus-negative presentation

The virtual class (1.7) does not depend on the choice of $n_i^+$ and $n_i^-$ used to represent
$n_i$. Theorem 7.1 extracts the unique irreducible class with that virtual character. Therefore
$r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}$ is independent of the presentation. The noncanonical cancellation
isomorphism (7.5) may change, but the remaining irreducible isomorphism class cannot.

### 11.2 Independence under conjugating a Brauer term

Replace $(H_i,\theta_i,\pi_{H_i})$ by

$$
(xH_ix^{-1},{}^x\theta_i,{}^x\pi_{H_i}).
\tag{11.1}
$$

The induction isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{xF_i}}^{G_{\mathbf Q}}
({}^xr_{H_i,\tau}\otimes{}^x\theta_{i,\tau})
\simeq
\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{F_i}}^{G_{\mathbf Q}}
(r_{H_i,\tau}\otimes\theta_{i,\tau})
\tag{11.2}
$$

identifies the actual summand. Thus the virtual class and its effective constituent are
unchanged. At a local place, the same operation permutes the Mackey double cosets and the
places above the rational prime; norms and residue degrees are preserved.

### 11.3 Independence of the Brauer relation after scalar extension

Let $\mathfrak B$ and $\mathfrak B'$ be two integral Brauer data for $1_\Gamma$. Enlarge to a
Galois number field $K'$ containing both coefficient ledgers, and choose one embedding
$\tau:K'\hookrightarrow\Omega$. Form the virtual difference

$$
\mathcal D_\tau=
\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B,\tau}-\mathcal R_{\mathfrak B',\tau}.
\tag{11.3}
$$

Apply the Gram calculation to the union of the two finite lists of induced summands, with
coefficient vector $(n_i,-n'_j)$. All cross inner products are again computed on elementary
intersections. Book 183's Proposition 6.2 applies to every cross pair, including non-Galois
intersection fields, and retains the corresponding quotient-character ratio. Thus Theorem 6.1
applies to the enlarged Gram matrix at the selected $\nu\mid3$, and under the optional uniform
packet certificate away from residue characteristic two. It also applies at a
coefficient-two embedding if $(\mathrm G_2)$ is supplied for the union of the two lists; in
particular, $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ supplies this. It then shows that
$\|\mathcal D_\tau\|^2$ is independent of $\tau$ in that range. At the one common
distinguished embedding fixed in the coefficient ledger, both Brauer
expressions are $[\rho]$, and hence

$$
\|\mathcal D_{\tau_0}\|^2=0.
\tag{11.4}
$$

Therefore $\|\mathcal D_\tau\|^2=0$ throughout that range. Positive definiteness of the
integral character norm gives $\mathcal D_\tau=0$. The two effective irreducibles are
consequently isomorphic:

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\simeq r_{\mathfrak B',\tau}.
\tag{11.5}
$$

This proves semisimple relation-independence over an algebraically closed common coefficient
field in the asserted range and therefore relation-independence of the effective irreducible.
If, at a coefficient-two embedding, the two relations separately satisfy their minimal
norm-one gates but the union Gram condition has not been supplied, Theorem 7.1 first produces
two actual semisimple representations. Book 183's relation-independent good polynomials give
equal traces on the dense good Frobenius set, so continuity and Brauer--Nesbitt identify them.
Thus separate norm-one effectivity is also enough for relation independence, though it does not
prove norm zero for the virtual difference before the two constituents are constructed.

Book 183 already proves that the good virtual polynomials are relation-independent. The Gram
calculation gives the stronger equality of the full virtual classes from the entrywise overlap
export; the last argument identifies the actual irreducibles from the minimal gates. No new
Frobenius-density theorem is needed in either proof.

### 11.4 Independence of harmless coefficient enlargement

Let $K\subseteq K'$ be a finite enlargement containing the same data, and let $\tau'$ extend
$\tau$. Place the finitely many local fields of definition for the two ledgers in one common
finite overfield. Each induced summand for $\tau'$ is then the scalar extension of the same
packet summand used for $\tau$; enlarging the algebraic ledger has not selected a new packet.
Hence the two virtual classes agree after scalar extension. Their unique effective irreducible
representatives agree:

$$
r_{\mathfrak B,\tau'}
\simeq r_{\mathfrak B,\tau}\otimes_{\Omega}\Omega'
\tag{11.6}
$$

after placement in a common algebraically closed field.

This statement does not descend the matrices to the smaller completion. A trace field can be
smaller than a field of definition, and the obstruction is not visible in (11.6).

### 11.5 Coefficient-conjugation covariance

Return to $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$. Suppose the selected-three conclusion,
the optional uniform away-from-two conclusion, or the relevant coefficient-two gate applies.
The two effective representations constructed from
$(\mathfrak B^\sigma,\tau)$ and $(\mathfrak B,\tau\circ\sigma)$ are represented term by term by
the same scalar images: applying $\sigma$ first and then $\tau$ is applying
$\tau\circ\sigma$. Their virtual classes therefore agree, so effectivity gives

$$
r_{\mathfrak B^\sigma,\tau}
\simeq r_{\mathfrak B,\tau\circ\sigma}
\tag{11.7}
$$

after scalar extension. This proves covariance of isomorphism classes. It does not choose a
semilinear descent operator satisfying a cocycle law.

### 11.6 Independence not yet claimed

The following stronger assertions are deliberately reserved.

- We have not chosen one minimal number field of coefficients and a model over each of its
  completions.
- We have not shown that the choices of local models and embeddings form one strictly compatible
  family before passage to algebraic closures.
- We have not produced coefficient descent data or proved the vanishing of a Schur-index or
  semilinear cocycle obstruction.
- We have not chosen stable lattices compatibly in $\tau$, and relation-independence has not been
  promoted to an integral isomorphism.
- We have not rebuilt every bad-place local--global compatibility statement for the newly
  assembled base-field family.

Book 185 begins with the effective isomorphism classes, the common polynomials, and the
independence results above. It then performs this coefficient and system-level assembly. Calling
the collection in this chapter the finished compatible system would erase that remaining work.

## 12. The dense-Frobenius rank-two criterion

### 12.1 The recognition problem

The final criterion is small but repeatedly useful. Let $F$ be a number field, let $q$ be a
prime, and let

$$
r:G_F\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(L)
\tag{12.1}
$$

be a continuous representation over a finite extension $L/\mathbf Q_q$. Let
$\chi_q:G_F\to L^\times$ be the $q$-adic cyclotomic character. Suppose $r$ and $\chi_q$ are
unramified at the places in a set $\Sigma$, and the Frobenius conjugacy classes from $\Sigma$
are dense in $G_F$.

Assume that arithmetic Frobenius satisfies

$$
\operatorname{tr}r(\operatorname{Frob}_v)
=1+\chi_q(\operatorname{Frob}_v)
\qquad(v\in\Sigma).
\tag{12.2}
$$

At a place $v\nmid q$ of residue cardinality $Nv$, this reads
$\operatorname{tr}r(\operatorname{Frob}_v)=1+Nv$. When $F=\mathbf Q$ and $v=p$, it is
$\operatorname{tr}r(\operatorname{Frob}_p)=1+p$.
The normalization matters: geometric Frobenius would replace $Nv$ by $(Nv)^{-1}$, and hence
$p$ by $p^{-1}$ over $\mathbf Q$.

### 12.2 Proof by Brauer–Nesbitt

The representation

$$
1\oplus\chi_q
\tag{12.3}
$$

is continuous and semisimple, with trace $1+\chi_q$. Both trace functions are continuous.
Equation (12.2) and density therefore give

$$
\operatorname{tr}r(g)=1+\chi_q(g)
\qquad(g\in G_F).
\tag{12.4}
$$

Apply Theorem 10.1.

**Theorem 12.1 (rank-two dense-Frobenius criterion).** Under the preceding hypotheses,

$$
\boxed{r^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq1\oplus\chi_q.}
\tag{12.5}
$$

In particular $r$ has reducible semisimplification.

**Proof.** Equations (12.3)--(12.4) give equality of traces of two existing representations on
all of $G_F$. Brauer--Nesbitt identifies their semisimplifications. $\square$

No determinant hypothesis is needed for the recognition once the full trace identity (12.2)
is known on a dense set. In the applications of this spine, the independently tracked
determinant $\det r=\chi_q$ is nevertheless an essential consistency check: it gives the same
determinant as (12.3) and prevents a normalization error from being hidden in the trace formula.

### 12.3 What the criterion does and does not recover

If $r$ is already semisimple, (12.5) is an isomorphism $r\simeq1\oplus\chi_q$. If $r$ is not
semisimple, it may be a nonsplit extension

$$
0\longrightarrow\chi_q\longrightarrow r\longrightarrow1\longrightarrow0
\tag{12.6}
$$

or the reverse extension, and both have the same trace as the split sum. Brauer--Nesbitt cannot
distinguish them. A stable lattice can introduce still further residual extension choices. The
criterion therefore says exactly “reducible semisimplification,” not “split representation.”

Conversely, an identity only for finitely many Frobenius elements is insufficient. Continuity
extends equality from a dense subset, not from an arbitrary infinite subset. The density
hypothesis must be verified in the arithmetic application.

### 12.4 Use as an irreducibility obstruction

Suppose a rank-two semisimple representation is known to be absolutely irreducible. Then it
cannot satisfy (12.2) on a dense Frobenius set, since Theorem 12.1 would make it reducible. In
particular, for the effective members of Theorem 7.1, the common trace system cannot equal
$1+\chi_\tau$ on a dense good set. At the distinguished member this is already visible from
the absolute irreducibility of $\rho$; the optional uniform packet certificate, together with
$(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ at coefficient characteristic two, makes the same obstruction available at
every embedding.

The remaining coefficient descent, coherent place indexing, model selection, and system-level

### 13.3 How the effectivity argument fits together

The construction combines general representation theory with the particular packet geometry
of Book 183. Continuous induction from open subgroups and the topology of finite-dimensional
coefficient spaces ensure that every induced term is a continuous representation. The Hom
pairing, Frobenius reciprocity, Mackey decomposition, Clifford blocks, and the norm-one sign
lemma then turn effectivity into a finite calculation. The exact intersection theorem of Book
183 supplies the arithmetic content of that calculation: over each possibly nonnormal
intersection field, both restrictions become twists of the same selected packet realization.

Chapters 5--6 identify the resulting Hom space with the correctly oriented finite-order
self-twist and compare its dimension with the distinguished realization. At the selected place
$\nu\mid3$ this gives the complete Gram matrix and hence norm one. Under the optional uniform
packet certificate the same comparison works away from coefficient characteristic two; the
separate coefficient-two hypotheses serve only the stronger all-place form. Once norm one is
known, positivity produces the actual constituent, and Chapters 7--9 transfer the determinant,
Frobenius polynomial, purity, and local SP object from the signed Mackey expression to that
constituent.

This order explains the role of density. Frobenius density recognizes representations that
already exist; it does not create the effective constituent. It also explains the remaining
coefficient boundary. The present argument gives isomorphism classes after scalar extension
and proves relation independence at that level. Descending those classes to completions of one
number field, indexing them coherently by its places, and choosing integral lattices are
different operations. In particular, the selected member at $\nu\mid3$ is effective without
any coefficient-two hypothesis, whereas an optional all-place assembly must retain its stated
coefficient-two gate.

### 13.5 Conclusion

The obstruction left by Brauer induction was not a missing Euler-factor calculation. Book 183
already had the correct quadratic factors, determinant line, twists, residue degrees, local
records, and distinguished member. The obstruction was positivity: subtraction in a character
ring had to be shown to cancel the same constituents everywhere at once.

Mackey theory found the correct places to test that cancellation. Frobenius reciprocity changed
an inner product of large induced representations into Hom spaces over elementary intersection
fields. The selected packet on each intersection made the two sides twists of one irreducible
rank-two realization at the selected place; the optional uniform packet certificate gives the
same input away from coefficient residue characteristic two, and $(\mathrm{AI}_2)$ supplies
the coefficient-two simplicity needed for the optional all-place form. Strong multiplicity one
made the resulting self-twist indicator algebraic and hence stable throughout the asserted
range. For a fixed coefficient-two relation, $(\mathrm G_2(\mathfrak B))$ is the finite termwise Hom
assertion that forces the same Gram entries, while $(\mathrm N_2(\mathfrak B))$ asks only for
the signed quadratic value needed by positivity.

At the distinguished place, the virtual class was the original irreducible representation, so
the fixed Gram calculation had norm one. Integrality then left no room for a hidden positive and
negative pair: one irreducible occurred with coefficient $1$ or $-1$, and dimension two chose
the positive sign. The virtual determinant became an actual cyclotomic determinant, the virtual
quadratic polynomial became an actual Frobenius polynomial, and the local Mackey record became
the record of an actual rank-two representation. At $2$, Krull--Schmidt cancellation retained
the nonzero rank-one monodromy, invariant line, Frobenius sign, and conductor one of the SP
pair whenever two is different from the coefficient residue characteristic. No such local
description was inferred at the coefficient prime.

Book 180's Brauer--Nesbitt theorem then supplied recognition, not existence. It proved the
semisimple independence statements that properly belong at this stage and gave the sharp
rank-two diagnostic: a dense trace identity $1+\chi$ forces semisimplification
$1\oplus\chi$. Book 183's completed intersection-field theorem closes the overlap
prerequisite. At the selected place $\nu\mid3$, its packet certificate supplies every
irreducibility and signed-special input needed for the norm-one argument, so Corollary 13.1A is
complete without a coefficient-two member or a uniform packet theorem.

The coefficient-two gates in Chapter 5 concern only an optional all-coefficient extension.
They do not limit the fixed-three effectivity result. Coherent coefficient descent and the
selected base-field assembly remain the work of the next book; integral refinement remains a
separate later construction.
