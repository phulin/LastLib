## 1. The attachment problem and its exact range

### 1.1 From Hecke scalars to Galois matrices

A parallel-weight-two eigenpacket supplies algebraic scalars at almost every finite place. In the unnormalized convention used here, the data at a spherical place $v$ are two eigenvalues $t_v$ and $s_v$ and the polynomial

$$
P_v(X)=X^2-t_vX+q_vs_v, \tag{1.1}
$$

where $q_v$ is the residue cardinality. The attachment problem asks for a continuous two-dimensional Galois representation on which a specified Frobenius has this characteristic polynomial.

The word “specified” carries real mathematical content. One must fix the ground field of the Galois group, arithmetic or geometric Frobenius, the action on Tate twists, and the orientation of the Hecke correspondence. One must also distinguish the field generated by the good eigenvalues from a field over which the complete finite Hecke block splits. Ignoring any one of these distinctions can reverse the central character, put $q_v$ in the denominator, or produce a representation of the wrong dimension.

Existence does not follow from the polynomials alone. The construction used in this volume is geometric: a compact Shimura curve has first etale cohomology with commuting Galois and Hecke actions. Automorphic decomposition identifies the packet block, and a multiplicity Hom space removes the finite-level automorphic factor. The two Hodge lines left over have dimension two.

### 1.2 The proved curve range

Let $F$ be totally real, choose a real place $\tau_0$, and let $B/F$ be a quaternion division algebra split at exactly $\tau_0$ and ramified at every other real place. The basic group is

$$
G=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbf Q}B^\times. \tag{1.2}
$$

For a suitable compact open $K$, the established canonical model is a smooth projective Shimura curve $C_K/E$, where the reflex field $E$ is the embedded copy $\tau_0(F)$. We identify $E$ with $F$ through $\tau_0$ only after recording this fact. The Galois representation constructed from the curve is initially a representation of $G_E$; it is not automatically a representation over an unrelated field bearing the same abstract degree.

The automorphic input in the proved range is a noncharacter parallel-weight-two representation $\Pi$ of $B^\times(\mathbf A_F)$ occurring in constant-coefficient $H^1(C_K)$. “Parallel weight two” here is intrinsic to $B^\times$: the active split real factor is the minimal cohomological discrete series, and the compact adjoint factors act trivially. No global transfer is needed to state or prove the curve theorem.

The basic group has an established polarized component construction. A connected central modification is covered only when its actual abelian quotient, finite contragredient twist, component covariance, and symmetric finite-module pairing have separately been supplied. Equality of adjoint groups is not enough. The main theorem below is stated for the basic group; the qualified central-modification variant follows by retaining exactly that additional determinant datum.

### 1.3 The automorphic input used by the FLT route

The FLT blueprint uses a different inner form. Its automorphic forms live on the units of a totally definite quaternion algebra $D/F$, with $[F:\mathbf Q]$ even, $D$ ramified at every real place and at no finite place, parallel weight two, trivial character, and square-free level $U_1(S)$. In this normalization “weight two” is the trivial compact type at infinity. At a place of $S$, the local representation allowed by the blueprint is either Steinberg or a tamely ramified principal series $\pi(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ with $\xi_1\xi_2$ unramified.

The selected global Jacquet--Langlands theorem first transfers a noncharacter packet on this totally definite $D^\times$ to a cuspidal parallel-weight-two representation $\pi$ of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf A_F)$ and preserves its good Hecke polynomials. Book 104, Theorem 9.2 then supplies the required one-split inner form whenever one can choose a finite set $W$ such that $(d-1)+|W|$ is even and every $\pi_w$, for $w\in W$, is selected special or selected depth-zero or tame positive-depth dihedral. It constructs the unique quaternionic packet for the algebra ramified at every real place except the chosen active place and exactly at $W$, without using curve cohomology.

Thus the curve carrier is unconditional in this parity-compatible selected range. In even degree one needs an odd nonempty eligible set. In odd degree at least three, $W=\varnothing$ works, so no eligible finite place is needed. Equality of almost all spherical eigenvalues still does not manufacture the finite ramification required in the even-degree case.

### 1.4 The logical order

Within the proved curve range, the argument has the following order.

1. Use the full finite Hecke image to isolate the rational packet block.
2. Extend the coefficient field enough to split its simple algebra.
3. Remove the finite Hecke module by a Hom construction and prove that the multiplicity is two.
4. Take the canonical semisimplification if a semisimple representation is required.
5. Factor Poincare duality through the symmetric finite-module pairing to determine the determinant independently.
6. At a genuinely good place, combine the determinant with the geometric quadratic relation to obtain the characteristic polynomial, including scalar Frobenius.
7. Repeat the construction at every coefficient place to obtain the common good polynomials.

The order prevents three circular arguments. The degree of an annihilating polynomial is not used to prove dimension two. The constant coefficient of that same annihilator is not used to invent the global determinant. A rational packet projector is not declared integral before its denominators have been controlled.

## 2. Coefficients and normalizations

### 2.1 Three coefficient fields

There are three fields to distinguish.

First, the good eigenvalues generate

$$
K_{\Pi}^{\mathrm{good}}
=\mathbf Q\bigl(t_v(\Pi),s_v(\Pi):v\notin S\bigr), \tag{2.1}
$$

with the finite central and component values adjoined when they are not already fixed. These numbers are algebraic because they occur as eigenvalues of a finite rational correspondence algebra.

Second, let $K_\Pi$ be the center of the simple factor of the full finite Hecke image which contains $\Pi$. The full image includes the level, central, and component operators needed to distinguish the packet. Thus $K_\Pi$ contains $K_\Pi^{\mathrm{good}}$, but equality is not asserted merely from the displayed good values.

Third, a simple factor over $K_\Pi$ can be a matrix algebra over a division algebra. Choose an embedding

$$
\iota:K_\Pi\hookrightarrow L \, \tag{2.2}
$$

into a number field which splits the selected central-simple factor and contains the values of the component character. Scalar extension of the rational orbit block can be a product of conjugate matrix factors; the embedding $\iota$ selects one of them. For a finite place $\lambda$ of $L$, write $L_\lambda$ for the completion and $\mathcal O_\lambda$ for its valuation ring.

The rank-two representation is constructed over $L_\lambda$. It is not silently descended to $K_\Pi^{\mathrm{good}}$. Before splitting, the invariant multiplicity object is a Morita module for the Schur division algebra rather than an ordinary two-dimensional vector space over its center.

### 2.2 The raw Hecke polynomial

At a split hyperspecial place $v$, set

$$
T_v=[K_v\operatorname{diag}(\varpi_v,1)K_v],
\qquad
S_v=[K_v\varpi_v I K_v]. \tag{2.3}
$$

These are unnormalized right-double-coset operators. If $KaK=\coprod_i a_iK$, the action is the sum $f(g)\mapsto\sum_i f(ga_i)$, with no division by the degree. The geometric span is oriented so that pullback along the translated source leg followed by trace along the forgetful target leg realizes this right action.

On the packet, $T_v$ and $S_v$ act by $t_v$ and $s_v$. The central value $s_v$ is not automatically $1$. For the trivial-character FLT input it is $1$ at every good place, but retaining $s_v$ until that specialization is made is the safest way to audit duals and twists.

### 2.3 Geometric Frobenius and Tate twists

Galois acts arithmetically. For a finite residue field of cardinality $q_u$, arithmetic Frobenius acts on the residue field by $x\mapsto x^{q_u}$, and geometric Frobenius is its inverse. We write $\Phi_u$ for geometric Frobenius and normalize the cyclotomic character by

$$
\chi_\lambda(\Phi_u)=q_u^{-1}. \tag{2.4}
$$

Thus $L_\lambda(1)$ has geometric-Frobenius eigenvalue $q_u^{-1}$, while $L_\lambda(-1)$ has eigenvalue $q_u$. By the curve case of the Weil bounds proved in Book 37, untwisted $H^1$ of a smooth projective curve is pure of weight one: its geometric-Frobenius roots have complex absolute value $q_u^{1/2}$.

At corresponding places $u$ of the reflex field and $v$ of $F$, the simple polynomial (1.1) is asserted only when the local reflex identification gives equal residue fields. If the residue degree is $f$, restriction replaces roots $\alpha_v,\beta_v$ by $\alpha_v^f,\beta_v^f$; it does not leave the same polynomial unchanged.

### 2.4 The covariant Tate-module conversion

Let $J_K$ be the product of the Jacobians of the geometric components of $C_K$. The covariant convention gives

$$
T_\lambda J_K\otimes L_\lambda
\simeq H^1(C_{K,\overline E},L_\lambda(1)). \tag{2.5}
$$

Suppose geometric Frobenius on untwisted $H^1$ has polynomial

$$
X^2-t_vX+q_vs_v. \tag{2.6}
$$

Arithmetic Frobenius on the same untwisted space has reciprocal roots and polynomial

$$
X^2-\frac{t_v}{q_vs_v}X+\frac{1}{q_vs_v}. \tag{2.7}
$$

After passing to the covariant Tate module, the twist multiplies those arithmetic roots by $q_v$ in the equal-residue-field case. The resulting polynomial is

$$
X^2-s_v^{-1}t_vX+q_vs_v^{-1}. \tag{2.8}
$$

Accordingly, if $\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}$ denotes the untwisted cohomological representation constructed below, put

$$
\rho^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\Pi,\lambda}
=\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(1). \tag{2.9}
$$

This is the covariant Tate-module normalization. When $s_v=1$, (2.6) and (2.8) happen to have the same written coefficients. That coincidence does not identify the two realizations: the first uses geometric Frobenius on untwisted cohomology, while the second uses arithmetic Frobenius after the Tate twist. The FLT definition uses the second convention.

## 3. The compact curve carrier

### 3.1 The one-split quaternion algebra

Fix $B/F$ and $C_K/E$ as in Section 1.2. The division hypothesis makes the Shimura curve proper: there are no rational parabolic subgroups and hence no cusps. A full component-routing orbit is used, so both Galois reciprocity and every Hecke correspondence under consideration preserve the selected union.

At non-neat level, characteristic-zero cohomology is obtained from a normal neat cover by pullback and trace. The division by the effective deck-group order is legitimate rationally. It need not be legitimate over $\mathcal O_\lambda$ when $\lambda$ divides that order, so no integral conclusion will be inferred from this rational descent.

The curve has constant-coefficient first cohomology

$$
H_\lambda
=H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(C_{K,\overline E},L_\lambda), \tag{3.1}
$$

with continuous $G_E$-action. Every descended Hecke correspondence acts on $H_\lambda$ and commutes with $G_E$.

### 3.2 The automorphic packet block

Let $e_{[\Pi]}$ be the central idempotent of the rational conjugacy-orbit block. After applying the center embedding $\iota$ of (2.2), let $e_{\Pi,\iota}$ denote the selected split matrix factor. The compact automorphic decomposition and the archimedean relative-cohomology calculation give

$$
e_{\Pi,\iota}\bigl(e_{[\Pi]}H_\lambda\bigr)
\simeq P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}\otimes_{L_\lambda}W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}, \tag{3.2}
$$

where $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ is the simple finite Hecke module for that matrix factor and

$$
\dim_{L_\lambda}W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}=2. \tag{3.3}
$$

Quaternionic automorphic multiplicity is one. The two dimensions in (3.3) come from the holomorphic and antiholomorphic cohomology lines at the unique split real place. If $\dim P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}=r$, then the selected split packet factor has dimension $2r$. Oldvectors enlarge $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$, not the Galois rank.

Norm characters do not occur in $H^1$. They are trivial on the active derived real group and contribute in degrees zero and two. Their exclusion is an archimedean cohomology calculation, not an appeal to the absence of cusps.

The embedding $\iota$ is fixed for the rest of the construction. To keep the formulas readable, subscripts $\iota$ are suppressed after Chapter 4; changing $\iota$ is treated separately in Section 7.2.

### 3.3 Components and descent

The full finite Hecke image must retain central and component-routing operators. A single geometric component need not be defined over $E$, and $S_v$ can move one component to another. Restricting to a component without checking both reciprocity stability and Hecke stability would make the claimed endomorphism ill typed.

For the basic group, quaternion conjugation identifies the contragredient packet with a finite reduced-norm twist. After passing to a fine level on which that twist is trivial, multiplication by the corresponding component character gives a self-transpose correspondence. Its Galois covariance defines a finite character

$$
\eta_\Pi:G_E\longrightarrow L^\times. \tag{3.4}
$$

At a good equal-residue-field place, the normalization is

$$
\eta_\Pi(\Phi_u)=s_v(\Pi). \tag{3.5}
$$

This equality is a theorem about reflex reciprocity and the chosen central generator. It is not a definition of $s_v$, and it is not available for an arbitrary inner form merely because the adjoint group looks the same.

### 3.4 The determinant datum

The determinant datum consists of two compatible pieces.

First, the component multiplier identifies the adjoint multiplicity space with

$$
W_{\Pi^\dagger,\lambda}
\simeq W_{\Pi,\lambda}\otimes\eta_\Pi^{-1} \tag{3.6}
$$

and has the Galois covariance encoded by (3.4). Second, the compact automorphic mass pairing gives a perfect symmetric form on the complete finite module $P_{\Pi,\lambda}$, including its oldvectors. Factoring the alternating curve polarization through this symmetric finite form leaves an alternating form on $W_{\Pi,\lambda}$.

For the basic group, these pieces have already been constructed at arbitrary characteristic-zero level by passing to a fine refinement and returning with pullback and trace. For a connected central modification they are hypotheses about the actual abelian quotient and component action. A bare adjoint pairing between $\Pi$ and $\Pi^\dagger$ is weaker and does not determine the determinant of $W_{\Pi,\lambda}$.

## 4. Extracting the rank-two factor

### 4.1 The finite Hecke image

Let $A_\lambda$ be the image of the full Hecke correspondence algebra in $\operatorname{End}_{L_\lambda}(H_\lambda)$. It is finite dimensional. Transpose correspondences make its complex realization stable under the adjoint for a positive Hodge form, so the image is semisimple. Write $A_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ for the matrix algebra selected by $e_{\Pi,\iota}$ inside the scalar extension of the rational block.

Using the image rather than the abstract Hecke algebra is essential. It removes operators which vanish on this cohomology while retaining every operator needed to distinguish the finite packet module. Using only spherical operators away from the level can leave several oldvectors, component characters, or nearly equivalent packets grouped together.

Because the correspondences descend to $E$, one has

$$
g(ah)=a(gh)
\qquad
(g\in G_E,\ a\in A_\lambda,\ h\in H_\lambda). \tag{4.1}
$$

Thus the rational block and its selected coefficient factor are Galois stable after scalar extension through $\iota$.

### 4.2 Multiplicity extraction

Choose a simple left module $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ for the split block and define

$$
W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}
=\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}}
\left(P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda},
e_{\Pi,\iota}\bigl(e_{[\Pi]}H_\lambda\bigr)\right). \tag{4.2}
$$

Galois acts by

$$
(g\cdot f)(p)=g(f(p)). \tag{4.3}
$$

Equation (4.1) shows that $g\cdot f$ is still $A_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$-linear.

**Proposition 4.1 (rank-two extraction).** Evaluation induces a Galois-equivariant isomorphism

$$
P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}\otimes_{L_\lambda}W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}
\overset\sim\longrightarrow
e_{\Pi,\iota}\bigl(e_{[\Pi]}H_\lambda\bigr),
\qquad p\otimes f\longmapsto f(p), \tag{4.4}
$$

and $W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ has dimension two.

**Proof.** After selecting $\iota$ and splitting, the chosen factor is a matrix algebra $M_d(L_\lambda)$ and $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ is its standard module. Every module for this factor is a direct sum of copies of $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$. If the number of copies is $m$, the Hom space in (4.2) is $L_\lambda^m$, and evaluation reconstructs the selected factor.

The automorphic decomposition computes $m=2$: global multiplicity is one, and the active real factor supplies exactly the two cohomology orientations. This proves the dimension and the evaluation isomorphism. Formula (4.3) makes evaluation Galois equivariant. $\square$

This proof does not choose an oldvector or a simultaneous Hecke eigenbasis. It also does not infer dimension from the degree of a Frobenius polynomial.

### 4.3 Continuity and semisimplification

The Hom space is cut out inside $\operatorname{Hom}_{L_\lambda}(P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda},H_\lambda)$ by finitely many closed linear equations expressing $A_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$-linearity. It is therefore a closed finite-dimensional subspace of a continuous representation, and (4.3) is continuous.

The raw cohomological representation need not have been proved semisimple. Define

$$
\rho_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}:G_E\longrightarrow
\operatorname{GL}\bigl(W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}^{\mathrm{ss}}\bigr) \tag{4.5}
$$

to be the direct sum of its Jordan--Holder factors. This representation is continuous, semisimple, and two-dimensional. Semisimplification preserves the characteristic polynomial of every group element, because the corresponding matrix is block upper triangular along a composition series.

Semisimplicity of (4.5) does not say that the raw extension splits. Nor does it say that the restriction to a decomposition group is semisimple in a way which forgets local monodromy. This book constructs the global semisimple representation and its good-place data only.

### 4.4 Change of coefficient field

If $L'/L$ is a finite extension carrying the chosen embedding $\iota$ and $\lambda'$ lies above $\lambda$, flat base change gives

$$
W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}\otimes_{L_\lambda}L'_{\lambda'}
\simeq W_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda'}. \tag{4.6}
$$

Changing the matrix splitting conjugates the split simple algebra by an inner automorphism and replaces $P_{\Pi,\iota,\lambda}$ by an isomorphic simple module. Composition with that isomorphism gives an isomorphic Hom space. Thus bases and matrix coordinates do not affect the isomorphism class.

This invariance does not prove descent through a nonsplit Schur algebra. The rational conjugacy-orbit block is defined before splitting; an individual two-dimensional vector-space realization is asserted only after the center embedding and splitting extension have been chosen. Changing $\iota$ can select a conjugate packet rather than another realization of the same labeled packet.

## 5. Good places and Frobenius

### 5.1 The complete good-place hypotheses

Let $u\nmid\ell$ be a finite place of $E$ corresponding to a finite place $v$ of $F$. The good-place polynomial is available only when all of the following hold:

- the local reflex identification gives the same residue field at $u$ and $v$;
- $B$ and the PEL endomorphism algebra are unramified at $v$;
- the level at $v$ is hyperspecial;
- the polarization lattice is self-dual and its polarization degree is prime to the residue characteristic;
- the determinant local model is the smooth one-dimensional unramified model;
- the neighboring-subgroup problem has the established rank-one incidence diagram;
- the relevant ordinary locus is dense on every selected component;
- the component union extends over the local base and is stable under $T_v$ and $S_v$;
- the level is neat, or descent from a neat cover is tame at the residue characteristic.

Under these hypotheses the curve has a smooth projective model, and the two normalized Hecke correspondences extend. Smooth proper base change kills inertia and identifies geometric Galois Frobenius with the special-fiber operator.

The assertions do not follow from the representation-theoretic word “unramified” alone. A bad determinant model, non-self-dual lattice, Iwahori level, residue-field mismatch, unstable component union, or wild coarse descent invalidates the displayed geometric relation.

### 5.2 The quadratic relation

On the full untwisted $H^1$, the special-fiber correspondence gives

$$
\Phi_u^2-T_v\Phi_u+q_vS_v=0. \tag{5.1}
$$

At a hyperspecial place, $T_v$ and $S_v$ are central in the local spherical algebra and commute with the other local factors. On the simple module they act by $t_v$ and $s_v$. Passing through the Hom construction yields

$$
\rho(\Phi_u)^2-t_v\rho(\Phi_u)+q_vs_v=0 \tag{5.2}
$$

on the raw multiplicity space and hence on its semisimplification.

Equation (5.2) proves unramifiedness and a quadratic annihilator. It does not yet prove the trace in the scalar case.

### 5.3 The scalar-Frobenius issue

An operator $cI$ is annihilated by every polynomial having $c$ as a root. Consequently, dimension two plus the degree-two relation (5.2) does not by itself make (1.1) the characteristic polynomial.

**Lemma 5.1.** Let $T$ be invertible on a two-dimensional vector space. If

$$
T^2-aT+dI=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\det T=d, \tag{5.3}
$$

then $\det(X-T)=X^2-aX+d$.

**Proof.** Cayley--Hamilton gives

$$
T^2-(\operatorname{tr}T)T+(\det T)I=0.
$$

Subtract (5.3). Since $T$ is invertible, $(\operatorname{tr}T-a)T=0$ implies $\operatorname{tr}T=a$. $\square$

The determinant in Lemma 5.1 will come from the global pairing in Chapter 6. This makes the scalar case noncircular: geometry supplies the relation, while duality supplies its constant term as an independently known determinant.

### 5.4 Purity at a good place

At a good place, $W_{\Pi,\lambda}$ is a multiplicity factor of smooth projective curve $H^1$. Book 37's curve Weil theorem therefore gives, for every complex embedding of each geometric-Frobenius root,

$$
|\iota(\alpha_u)|=q_u^{1/2}. \tag{5.4}
$$

This is purity of weight one at the good places. It is inherited from the curve and does not follow merely from the determinant, which controls only the product of the roots. When the characteristic polynomial is known, purity gives

$$
|\iota(t_v)|\le 2q_v^{1/2}. \tag{5.5}
$$

No purity statement at a bad place is inferred here from (5.4). Such a statement requires a separate analysis of nearby cycles and its monodromy filtration.

## 6. Determinant, duality, and parity

### 6.1 Factoring Poincare duality

Poincare duality on untwisted curve cohomology has target $L_\lambda(-1)$ and pairs the $\Pi$ block with its adjoint block. Under the determinant datum of Section 3.4, the component twist and the symmetric finite-module form factor the ambient alternating pairing as

$$
b_P\otimes\psi_\Pi,
$$

where $b_P$ is symmetric on $P_{\Pi,\lambda}$ and

$$
\psi_\Pi:
W_{\Pi,\lambda}\times W_{\Pi,\lambda}
\longrightarrow L_\lambda(-1)\otimes\eta_\Pi \tag{6.1}
$$

is perfect and alternating. The symmetry of $b_P$ is essential. If it were alternating, the tensor sign would leave a symmetric form on the multiplicity factor and would not determine the same rank-two similitude character.

The pairing remains valid at old level because the mass pairing is constructed on the complete finite module and returned from a fine level by pullback and trace. A pairing on a chosen newvector line would not justify the same conclusion for an arbitrary old packet.

### 6.2 The determinant formula

For every two-dimensional vector space $W$ and every alternating form $\psi$,

$$
\psi(gx,gy)=\det(g)\psi(x,y). \tag{6.2}
$$

The target of (6.1) has Galois character $\chi_\lambda^{-1}\eta_\Pi$. Hence

$$
\boxed{
\det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}
=\chi_\lambda^{-1}\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}.} \tag{6.3}
$$

This is an identity on all of $G_E$, not only on good Frobenius classes. It is unchanged by semisimplification or by rescaling the pairing.

The associated essential duality is

$$
\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}^\vee
\simeq
\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(1)\otimes\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}^{-1}. \tag{6.4}
$$

At a good equal-residue-field place, (2.4), (3.5), and (6.3) give

$$
\det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(\Phi_u)=q_vs_v. \tag{6.5}
$$

Combining (6.5), (5.2), and Lemma 5.1 proves

$$
\boxed{
\det\bigl(X-\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(\Phi_u)\bigr)
=X^2-t_vX+q_vs_v,} \tag{6.6}
$$

including when Frobenius is scalar.

For the trivial-character packet used by the FLT blueprint, $s_v=1$ and $\eta_\Pi=1$, so

$$
\det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}=\chi_\lambda^{-1},
\qquad
P_v(X)=X^2-t_vX+q_v. \tag{6.7}
$$

These formulas apply only after such a packet has actually entered the proved curve range.

For the covariant Tate-module normalization (2.9), twisting a rank-two representation changes its determinant by $\chi_\lambda^2$. Therefore

$$
\det\rho^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\Pi,\lambda}
=\chi_\lambda\eta_{\Pi,\lambda},
\qquad
\det\bigl(X-\rho^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\Pi,\lambda}
(\operatorname{Frob}^{\mathrm{arith}}_u)\bigr)
=X^2-s_v^{-1}t_vX+q_vs_v^{-1}. \tag{6.8}
$$

For the trivial-character FLT packet this becomes determinant $\chi_\lambda$ and arithmetic-Frobenius polynomial $X^2-t_vX+q_v$. Thus (6.8), rather than the untwisted determinant in (6.7), is the convention used by the FLT definition.

The essential duality (6.4) also gives $\rho^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\Pi,\lambda}\simeq\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}^\vee\otimes\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}$. In the trivial-character FLT range, the covariant Tate realization and the dual of the untwisted cohomological realization are therefore the same representation.

### 6.3 Total oddness

Let $c_\tau$ be complex conjugation at a real place of $E$. The component character constructed from a finite automorphic twist trivial at infinity is even:

$$
\eta_\Pi(c_\tau)=1. \tag{6.9}
$$

Since $\chi_\lambda(c_\tau)=-1$, the determinant formula gives

$$
\det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(c_\tau)=-1. \tag{6.10}
$$

Moreover $c_\tau^2=1$. In characteristic zero its eigenvalues lie in $\{1,-1\}$, and determinant $-1$ forces one of each. Thus

$$
\operatorname{tr}\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(c_\tau)=0. \tag{6.11}
$$

The representation is totally odd. This argument uses the established determinant datum and the evenness of its actual component character. Without that datum, the Hodge-line argument proves oddness only when the individual coefficient block is preserved by the real descent action. One must not infer preservation of a coefficient embedding merely because the rational conjugacy-orbit block is stable.

In residue characteristic two the two residual eigenvalues coincide. Total oddness is a characteristic-zero statement; it is not detected by the residual determinant alone.

### 6.4 Four normalization tests

The formulas can be checked without appealing to memory.

1. With trivial component character, geometric Frobenius on untwisted $H^1$ has determinant $q_v$, agreeing with $\chi_\lambda^{-1}(\Phi_v)$.
2. Arithmetic Frobenius on the same space has reciprocal roots and determinant $q_v^{-1}$; after the covariant Tate twist it has determinant $q_v$ and the FLT polynomial when the component character is trivial.
3. Twisting by a finite character $\xi$ multiplies $t_v$ by $\xi(\Phi_v)$, $s_v$ by $\xi(\Phi_v)^2$, and the determinant by $\xi^2$.
4. A Tate twist by $(n)$ multiplies geometric-Frobenius roots by $q_v^{-n}$ and the determinant character by $\chi_\lambda^{2n}$.

These tests distinguish four operations which are often accidentally merged: inversion of Frobenius, duality, a Tate twist, and a finite central twist.

## 7. The family over all coefficient places

### 7.1 Weak compatibility

Choose one center embedding $\iota:K_\Pi\hookrightarrow L$ and one global splitting field for its selected packet factor. For every finite place $\lambda$ of $L$, the same rational correspondence algebra and the same multiplicity calculation construct $\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}$. Let $S$ contain the finitely many places where the group, level, component union, character, model, correspondence, or equal-residue-field hypothesis fails.

Then $S$ is independent of $\lambda$, and for $v\notin S$ with $v\nmid\ell(\lambda)$ one has unramifiedness and the common polynomial (6.6) in $L[X]$. Thus

$$
\{\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}\}_\lambda \tag{7.1}
$$

is a weakly compatible rank-two family in the proved curve range. It is pure of weight one at its good places.

Weak compatibility compares algebraic polynomials before completion. It does not identify an $L_\lambda$-vector space with an $L_{\lambda'}$-vector space, and it says nothing at a place whose residue characteristic equals that of the chosen coefficient field.

The family does not require a density theorem for its construction: every member is extracted directly from the same rational curve block. A density theorem would be needed for some later uniqueness or comparison arguments, but no such argument is used as a proof input here.

### 7.2 Coefficient conjugation

An automorphism $\sigma$ of a normal splitting field sends the packet data to their conjugates:

$$
t_v\longmapsto\sigma(t_v),
\qquad
s_v\longmapsto\sigma(s_v),
\qquad
\eta_\Pi\longmapsto\sigma(\eta_\Pi). \tag{7.2}
$$

Applying $\sigma$ to the rational block and then completing gives the representation attached to the conjugate block. If $\sigma$ does not fix the packet, this is a representation attached to $\Pi^\sigma$, not a second realization of the unchanged labeled packet. All coefficients, including the component character, must be conjugated together.

The construction is compatible with extension of the splitting field by (4.6). It does not show that the rank-two representation descends to the good eigenvalue field, because the Schur obstruction can survive there.

### 7.3 Integral and local boundaries

Continuity gives some $G_E$-stable $\mathcal O_\lambda$-lattice in $W_{\Pi,\lambda}$. The rational packet block also has a natural saturated intersection with integral curve cohomology. Neither fact supplies a canonical rank-two lattice.

To remove $P_{\Pi,\lambda}$ integrally, one needs a split integral Hecke order, a projective generator, and an integral evaluation isomorphism. Rational Morita theory does not imply these conditions. Projector denominators, coarse-level deck orders, the discriminant of the Hecke order, and the index of the returned pairing are independent possible obstructions.

Likewise, a stable lattice does not by itself have finite-flat coefficient quotients at a place above $\ell$. Such a conclusion requires an actual good-reduction abelian carrier or a suitable integral low-weight comparison object, with the coefficient action and every base hypothesis stated. No finite-flat quotient is constructed in this book.

At a bad place away from $\ell$, the good polynomial does not determine inertia, monodromy, conductor, or the full Weil--Deligne parameter. No strict local--global compatibility statement is made here. These omissions are scope boundaries, not consequences hidden in the word “attached.”

## 8. The curve attachment theorem

### 8.1 Statement

**Theorem 8.1 (attachment in the proved one-split curve range).** Let $F$ be totally real, let $B/F$ be a quaternion division algebra split at exactly one real place $\tau_0$, and let $C_K/E$ be the compact Shimura curve for the basic group $\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbf Q}B^\times$, on a full Galois- and Hecke-stable component-routing orbit. Identify the reflex field $E=\tau_0(F)$ with $F$ through $\tau_0$. Let $\Pi$ be a noncharacter parallel-weight-two packet occurring in constant-coefficient $H^1(C_K)$.

Let $K_\Pi$ be the center of its simple full-Hecke block. Choose an embedding $\iota:K_\Pi\hookrightarrow L$ into a finite field which splits the selected central-simple factor and contains the component-character values. Then for every finite place $\lambda$ of $L$ there is a continuous semisimple representation

$$
\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}:G_F\longrightarrow
\operatorname{GL}_2(L_\lambda) \tag{8.1}
$$

with the following properties.

1. It is obtained from the two-dimensional multiplicity Hom space selected by $\iota$ and is independent, up to isomorphism and coefficient extension, of bases and matrix splittings. A different center embedding can select the conjugate packet.
2. Its determinant and duality are

   $$
   \det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}=\chi_\lambda^{-1}\eta_{\Pi,\lambda},
   \qquad
   \rho_{\Pi,\lambda}^\vee
   \simeq\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(1)\otimes\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}^{-1}.
   $$

3. It is totally odd.
4. There is a finite set $S$, independent of $\lambda$, such that at every $v\notin S$ with $v\nmid\ell(\lambda)$ satisfying the equal-residue-field identification, the representation is unramified and geometric Frobenius has characteristic polynomial

   $$
   X^2-t_v(\Pi)X+q_vs_v(\Pi).
   $$

5. The family over all $\lambda$ is weakly compatible and pure of weight one at these good places.
6. The covariant Tate realization $\rho^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\Pi,\lambda}=\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}(1)$ has determinant $\chi_\lambda\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}$ and the arithmetic-Frobenius polynomial (2.8). For trivial character it has determinant $\chi_\lambda$ and polynomial $X^2-t_vX+q_v$, exactly the FLT convention.
7. A stable lattice exists. No canonical rank-two integral lattice, finite-flat quotient, bad-place Weil--Deligne comparison, or independence from an unrelated geometric carrier is asserted.

For a connected central modification, the same conclusion holds only after replacing the automatic basic-group determinant construction by the explicit determinant datum of Sections 3.3--3.4 and retaining the actual reflex field as the ground field of the Galois representation.

Moreover, let $d=[F:\mathbf Q]\ge2$, fix $\tau_0$, and let $\pi$ be a cuspidal full-group parallel-weight-two representation of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf A_F)$ with compatible unitary central character. Within the selected local range of Book 104, a compact curve carrier exists unconditionally whenever there is a finite set $W$ such that $(d-1)+|W|$ is even and $\pi_w$ is selected special or selected tame dihedral for every $w\in W$. In that case Book 104, Theorem 9.2 supplies the unique packet $\Pi_B$ for the quaternion algebra ramified at every real place except $\tau_0$ and at exactly $W$; after choosing $K$ with $\Pi_{B,f}^K\ne0$, all conclusions above apply to $\Pi_B$. Thus in even degree this is the old odd-nonempty eligible condition, while in odd degree one may take $W=\varnothing$ and every such split packet has a compact basic curve carrier.

### 8.2 Proof

The automorphic decomposition of the compact curve gives the split block (3.2), exact multiplicity two, Hodge types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, and the full finite Hecke module. The descended correspondences commute with $G_E$. Proposition 4.1 therefore constructs the raw continuous rank-two Hom space, and Jordan--Holder semisimplification gives (8.1).

For the basic group, the component multiplier, its reflex-reciprocity covariance, the symmetric oldspace mass pairing, and the returned Poincare-compatible correspondence supply (6.1). The rank-two alternating-similitude identity gives the determinant and duality. The component character is even at the real places, so Section 6.3 gives total oddness.

Choose $S$ to contain every place excluded by the good-place list. Smooth proper base change gives unramifiedness outside $S$ and away from the coefficient residue characteristic. The special-fiber correspondence gives (5.2), while the determinant gives (6.5). Lemma 5.1 produces the characteristic polynomial even for scalar Frobenius.

All of these operations are defined from one rational Hecke block, one center embedding, and one global splitting field. Repeating them at every completion gives weak compatibility. Book 37's curve Weil theorem gives weight one at the good places. Twisting by $L_\lambda(1)$ gives part 6 by (2.8) and (6.8). Finally, compactness of the image of a continuous representation gives a stable lattice. Section 7.3 explains why none of the stronger integral or bad-place conclusions follows. The moreover clause is Book 104, Theorem 9.2 followed by this construction; Section 8.3 checks its exact image and the noncircular dependency order. $\square$

### 8.3 The exact FLT bridge and the eligibility boundary

Let $F$ have even degree and let $D/F$ be the totally definite quaternion algebra ramified at every real place and no finite place, as in the FLT blueprint. Let $\Pi_D$ be a noncharacter weight-two packet with trivial character and square-free level $U_1(S)$ in the selected global Jacquet--Langlands range. The established transfer gives a cuspidal parallel-weight-two representation

$$
\pi=\operatorname{JL}_D(\Pi_D)
\quad\text{of}\quad
\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf A_F), \tag{8.2}
$$

with the same good polynomial $X^2-t_vX+q_v$.

Call a finite place $w$ **eligible** if $\pi_w$ is selected special or selected depth-zero or tame positive-depth dihedral in the exact range of Books 80 and 103--104. To apply Theorem 8.1, choose an odd nonempty finite set $W$ of eligible places and let $B/F$ be ramified at every real place except $\tau_0$ and exactly at $W$.

Ramification parity requires $|W|$ to be odd because $B$ is ramified at $[F:\mathbf Q]-1$ real places. Book 104, Theorem 9.2 then constructs, unconditionally and uniquely, the noncharacter packet

$$
\Pi_B=\operatorname{JL}_B^{-1}(\pi), \tag{8.3}
$$

with the required parallel-weight-two infinity type, multiplicity one, central character, and good right-convolution Hecke polynomials. Choosing a compact open $K$ with $\Pi_{B,f}^K\ne0$ places this packet in the automorphic decomposition of the compact Shimura curve, so Theorem 8.1 attaches its Galois family. The construction is not circular: the proof of Book 104, Theorem 9.2 uses compact automorphic traces, local transfer, and the split trace formula, but not Shimura-curve cohomology or a Galois representation attached here.

In the square-free FLT specialization stated in Section 1.3, a special place is a Steinberg place, while a tame principal-series place is not eligible. Hence any nonempty set of Steinberg places contains an odd nonempty choice $W$ (for example a singleton), and the curve carrier is unconditional. If there is no Steinberg place, there is no eligible place in that specialization and no such $W$; this is the genuine remaining boundary. More generally, selected tame-dihedral discrete-series places are also eligible, but principal-series, primitive wild, and excluded dyadic places are not added to the range.

For every permitted $W$, the untwisted cohomological family has determinant $\chi_\lambda^{-1}$ and is totally odd. Its covariant Tate realization has determinant $\chi_\lambda$ and arithmetic-Frobenius polynomial $X^2-t_vX+q_v$, the exact FLT normalization. After extending $L_\lambda$ to the chosen algebraic closure, it has the coefficient type used in the blueprint.

The FLT blueprint also mentions compact Shimura surfaces as another possible geometric carrier. No prior book proves the required automorphic decomposition of surface $H^2$, isolates the relevant two-dimensional multiplicity factor, or checks its determinant and Frobenius normalization. This book makes no surface claim and does not use a surface to replace the selected curve carrier above.

## 9. Dependency audit and conclusion

### 9.1 Exact proof dependencies

The proofs in this book use exactly the following prior-book outputs.

**Book 125, Automorphic Decomposition of Shimura-Curve $H^1$.** It supplies the compact one-split curve, its component descent, the common geometric and adelic Hecke action, exact noncharacter parallel-weight-two decomposition, multiplicity one, the finite semisimple Hecke image, coefficient and Schur-field distinctions, the rank-two multiplicity calculation, Hodge types, the basic-group determinant datum, the complete good-place relation, and the saturated ambient packet lattice with its denominator warnings.

**Book 126, Galois Representations from Weight-Two Shimura-Curve Cohomology.** It supplies the continuous Hom extraction, canonical semisimplification, coefficient-field behavior, factored determinant and duality, parity, the scalar-Frobenius upgrade, and the boundary between rational extraction and integral Morita theory.

**Book 37, Weights and Weil Bounds for Curves and Abelian Varieties.** Its curve Weil theorem supplies purity of smooth proper $H^1$ and hence the weight-one bounds for the extracted multiplicity factor. Book 126 deliberately does not prove or import this purity statement.

**Book 104, Global Jacquet--Langlands.** Its totally definite theorem supplies the split cusp form (8.2). For $[F:\mathbf Q]\ge2$, a compatible unitary central character, and one active real place, its compact one-split inverse theorem supplies the inverse packet when the selected finite ramification set $W$ satisfies $(d-1)+|W|$ even. It preserves the central character, multiplicity, and good right-convolution Hecke polynomials. For odd $d$ the empty set is allowed, so no eligible finite place is needed; for even $d$ this specializes to the odd-nonempty condition used in (8.3). Its proof does not use Shimura-curve cohomology, Book 125, Book 126, or the Galois extraction of this book.

Finite-dimensional semisimple algebra, Jordan--Holder theory, Cayley--Hamilton, and the existence of a stable lattice for a continuous representation of a profinite group are ordinary foundational inputs. No density theorem, later local--global compatibility theorem, compatible-system theorem, or finite-flat quotient theorem is used in a proof.

The dependency order is therefore Book 104's trace-theoretic construction of $\Pi_B$, Book 125's decomposition of the resulting curve packet, Book 126's cohomological extraction, Book 37's independent purity theorem, and finally the assembly in Theorem 8.1. No step uses the desired Galois representation to prove the preceding automorphic transfer, so the argument is noncircular.

### 9.2 Claims deliberately not made

The scope can be summarized without ambiguity.

- No representation is attached here to an arbitrary inner form of $\operatorname{GL}_2$.
- No modular-curve branch is claimed: the present proof dependencies do not supply the full packet multiplicity and determinant construction for that carrier.
- No Shimura-surface cohomology claim is made.
- In even degree, no compact curve carrier is asserted when the split packet has no eligible selected special or selected tame-dihedral finite place. In odd degree at least three, the empty finite ramification set already gives the basic compact carrier.
- No strict compatibility at bad places or at the coefficient residue characteristic is claimed.
- No canonical integral rank-two lattice or finite-flat quotient is constructed.
- No comparison of unrelated carriers is deduced from good polynomials.

These exclusions leave one coherent result: attachment from a one-split compact curve packet to its rank-two weakly compatible Galois family, with Book 104 supplying that curve packet from every parity-compatible selected set $W$. This requires an odd nonempty eligible set in even degree and permits $W=\varnothing$ in odd degree.

### 9.3 Conclusion

In the proved curve range, the passage from automorphic forms to Galois representations is complete and normalized. The full finite Hecke block keeps components and oldvectors visible; a splitting field removes the Schur obstruction; and the multiplicity Hom space has rank two because the unique active real factor contributes one holomorphic and one antiholomorphic class. Poincare duality, the symmetric oldspace pairing, and the descended component character give

$$
\det\rho_{\Pi,\lambda}=\chi_\lambda^{-1}\eta_{\Pi,\lambda}.
$$

At a genuinely good place this independent determinant turns the geometric quadratic relation into the characteristic polynomial $X^2-t_vX+q_vs_v$, even for scalar Frobenius. The same rational block at every coefficient completion gives a pure weakly compatible family, and the even component character gives total oddness. Passing to the covariant Tate realization replaces the determinant by $\chi_\lambda\eta_\Pi$ and uses arithmetic Frobenius; for trivial character this is exactly the determinant and polynomial convention in the FLT blueprint.

For the FLT automorphic packet, the totally definite transfer first produces $\pi$. If $\pi$ has an odd nonempty finite set $W$ of selected special or selected tame-dihedral places, Book 104 produces the one-split packet and the compact curve carrier unconditionally; the theorem above then gives the normalized Galois family. In the square-free specialization, this is exactly the case in which a Steinberg place exists. If there is no eligible finite place, no such $W$ exists and the curve-carrier route genuinely stops before cohomological extraction. No surface theorem is invoked to alter that boundary.
